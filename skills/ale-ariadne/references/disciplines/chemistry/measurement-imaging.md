# Chemistry: measurement and imaging

This file adds content specific to measurement and imaging to `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: measurement and data interpretation in analytical chemistry: separation science, mass spectrometry, quantitative spectroscopy and chemometrics, surface and materials analysis, electroanalysis and sensing, chemical imaging and image reconstruction, and structure elucidation from NMR and mass-spectrometry data. For the theoretical assignment of molecular spectra and ultrafast spectroscopy see `structure-dynamics.md`; for polymer characterization see `macromolecular-nano.md`; for structure determination of biological macromolecules and omics mass spectrometry see `references/disciplines/biology.md`; for crystal diffraction and materials characterization see `references/disciplines/materials.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: inferring structures from measured spectra of compounds of known structure, where the answers can be checked one by one. An example is the elucidation of unknown organic compounds from high-resolution mass spectra and 1D and 2D NMR data: determining the molecular formula, identifying fragments, establishing connectivity and checking candidate structures back against predicted spectra are each served by mature programs and public spectral libraries.
- Build with priority: quantification and calibration problems with known true values, for example multivariate calibration of near-infrared spectra and calibration transfer between instruments, or prediction of chromatographic retention times. They are judged by the prediction error on held-out samples or on another instrument, with the tolerance set from the measurement error of the reference method.
- Build with priority: tasks whose verdict needs only one short measurement by a procedure already fixed (physical-world observation level 2), for example designing a chromatographic separation method for a given mixture, or predicting the spectrum of a compound on a specified instrument, measured in the expert's laboratory within six hours. The part that needs an experiment cannot be run on the expert's side and is handled according to "Tasks at physical-world observation level 2" in `references/2-research.md`: the check is written as usual, the part is recorded faithfully as not run, and no data may be fabricated.
- Defer: image reconstruction in cryo-electron microscopy and super-resolution imaging. The problem is purely algorithmic, but the raw data are large (an EMPIAR entry averages on the order of terabytes) and are hard to reprocess during review. Discuss with the expert the use of a downsampled subset, or of data whose true values are generated by simulation (for example the simulated datasets of the SMLM Challenge), judged by the deviation of the reconstruction from the true values.
- Defer: the development of new single-molecule and in situ measurement methods and new instruments, and tasks that can be judged only after a new sample is prepared or a new apparatus is built (physical-world observation level 3). The core of such work is the instrument; computation can only assess a design.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The form of the data: raw data (FIDs, profile-mode mass spectra) or processed peak lists; the acquisition conditions (solvent, field strength, temperature, ionization mode and adducts, collision energy, column and gradient).
- Instrument performance and noise level: mass accuracy and resolution, the measurement error of isotope abundances, how chemical shifts are referenced, the signal-to-noise ratio.
- The actual state of the sample: purity, whether it is a mixture, solvent and impurity peaks, whether tautomerism, conformational exchange or aggregation is present.
- What the true value rests on: comparison with a reference standard, independent synthesis, a single-crystal structure, or an assignment in the literature; whether that assignment has ever been revised.
- The form of the conclusion and the standard of equivalence: planar structure, relative configuration or absolute configuration; whether isomers the data cannot distinguish may be answered as a candidate set.
- The statement of identification confidence that the field accepts (for example the five confidence levels proposed by Schymanski et al. for identification by high-resolution mass spectrometry), and which level the task requires.
- For quantification tasks, the reference method and its uncertainty, the definitions of the limits of detection and quantification, the calibration range and the matrix.
- For tasks at physical-world observation level 2: who completes the measurement within six hours, on which instrument and by which method; the criterion must be written down before the measurement.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Back-check of the molecular formula | The exact mass and isotope pattern computed from the submitted formula deviate from the measurement by no more than the mass accuracy and isotope-abundance error of the instrument; valence rules and the degree of unsaturation are satisfied | Uses existing data, must pass |
| Back-check of a candidate structure against spectra | With a prediction program fixed in advance, the agent judge computes the ¹³C and ¹H chemical shifts of the submitted structure and the expected HSQC, HMBC and COSY correlations, and compares them with the measurement: shift deviations lie within the error of that prediction method, and every measured cross peak can be explained by the structure | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Checking against the known structures one by one | The submitted structure is compared with the true structure after canonicalization, with stereochemistry and tautomerism treated as the task statement specifies; when a candidate set is submitted, the score follows the rank of the true structure in it | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Discrimination between candidates | The submitter lists the other candidates compatible with the data and the grounds for excluding them; the agent judge enumerates candidates with a structure generator under the given formula and fragment constraints, and checks whether a structure remains that was not excluded and whose predicted spectra fit equally well | Pure computation, counts for part of the score |
| Quantification error on held-out samples | The root-mean-square error of prediction of the calibration model on held-out samples or on another instrument, compared with the uncertainty of the reference method and with a baseline model | Uses existing data |
| Reconstruction on simulated phantoms | An image reconstruction or peak-fitting program is run on data whose true values are generated by simulation; the agent judge reruns it and computes the deviation from the true values (localization error, detection rate, resolution) | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Comparison with a new measurement | The expert's laboratory completes one measurement by the submitted method or for the submitted prediction; the agent judge decides by a criterion written down in advance (resolution of the separation, retention-time deviation, the match between predicted and measured spectra). In the local build this item is recorded as not run | Needs a new observation (physical-world observation level 2) |

## Sources of existing data

- NMR: nmrshiftdb2 (an open database, data under an extended version of the Open Database License, with shift prediction based on HOSE codes), NP-MRD (natural products, free access, data under CC BY-NC 4.0), BMRB (biomolecules and metabolites). The terms of use differ between spectral libraries; check each one for permission to redistribute before placing its data in a task package.
- Mass spectrometry: the public tandem mass spectral libraries of MassBank, MoNA and GNPS, whose records each carry their own license and some of which forbid redistribution, so check them record by record. The NIST mass spectral library needs a commercial license and cannot be redistributed.
- Blind tests and benchmarks: CASMI (a blind contest in small-molecule identification; the automated-methods categories of 2016 had 208 challenges in total) and MassSpecGym (three kinds of task: generating structures from tandem mass spectra, retrieving structures, and simulating spectra). Their challenges and answers are all public.
- Quantification and separation: the near-infrared datasets published by Eigenvector (corn: 80 samples, 3 spectrometers; tablets: 654 tablets, 2 spectrometers); METLIN SMRT (reversed-phase retention times of 80,038 small molecules).
- Imaging: EMPIAR (raw cryo-electron microscopy images, CC0 license); the simulated datasets of the SMLM Challenge (with true values). Data whose true values are generated by simulation count as pure computation, not existing data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The structure of a known compound can be found from its molecular formula in PubChem, from its ¹³C shifts in nmrshiftdb2, and from its tandem mass spectrum in the public libraries. Use spectra of compounds the expert's laboratory has not yet published, or entries added after a split in time.
- When the task is set with simulated spectra of derivatives absent from the databases, it becomes pure computation; noise at the measured level, solvent peaks and impurity peaks must be added, and the task statement must say that the spectra are simulated.
- The answers of contests and benchmarks are public and may already be in the training data of public prediction tools. If they are reused as they stand, the score may reflect recall instead of interpretation.
- The metadata of raw data files often contain sample names, compound names and file paths. Remove them before the files go into the environment, and check the parameter files and title fields.
- The task statement contains no compound names, source organisms, paper titles or spectral library entry IDs. Information on the source narrows the candidates considerably; whether to provide it is for the expert to decide.

## Tools and compute

- NMR processing and prediction: nmrglue and NMRium (open source); MestReNova and ACD/Labs are commercial software, and the processing version of TopSpin is free for academic users on application. Shifts can be predicted with the HOSE-code predictor of nmrshiftdb2 or by GIAO calculations with DFT (ORCA, NWChem, Gaussian), with xtb and CREST for the conformer search.
- Structure elucidation and candidate assessment: Sherlock (open-source computer-assisted structure elucidation, which generates structures with pyLSD), the structure generators surge and MAYGEN, and the DP4 and DP5 probabilities (the open-source implementation is under the MIT license and calls Gaussian or NWChem for the quantum chemistry). ACD/Structure Elucidator and similar programs are commercial.
- Mass spectrometry: SIRIUS (with CSI:FingerID; the code is under the AGPL license, the web services are for academic use only and need an account and network access, and non-academic users must buy a separate license), MetFrag, CFM-ID, matchms, MZmine, OpenMS.
- Imaging and chemometrics: RELION (open source), cryoSPARC (free for academic users), ThunderSTORM; partial least squares regression in scikit-learn.
- Compute: for the spectral interpretation of a set of tens of unknowns, solving is on the order of hours on an ordinary computer; when chemical shifts are computed by DFT, each candidate structure takes a further few to a few tens of CPU core-hours. The review only predicts and compares for the submitted structures and is far smaller than solving. Cryo-electron microscopy reconstruction needs GPUs and storage on the order of terabytes. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- The molecular formula determined from mass accuracy alone. The calculations of Kind and Fiehn show that even a mass accuracy better than 1 ppm does not determine the formula uniquely; the isotope abundance pattern must be used as well.
- A structure declared correct because the residuals are at noise level. More than one structure may be compatible with all the spectra: the structure first assigned to hexacyclinol was later revised by Rychnovsky by comparison with computed ¹³C shifts, and the revision was confirmed by Porco's synthesis.
- Structures judged by exact string match. Tautomerism, unspecified stereocenters and salt forms make correct answers count as wrong; mass-spectrometry data usually cannot determine stereochemistry, and CASMI accordingly judges by the first block of the InChIKey only.
- A tolerance on predicted shifts set tighter than the error of the prediction method itself, or the same tolerance used for ¹³C and ¹H, or for HOSE-code prediction and DFT prediction.
- Every HMBC correlation treated as a two-bond or three-bond correlation. Four-bond correlations and missing cross peaks make the constraints too strict or too loose.
- The effect of solvent, concentration, pH and temperature on chemical shifts ignored, and literature data taken under different conditions compared directly.
- Adduct ions, in-source fragments and isotope peaks mistaken for the molecular ion, so that the molecular formula comes out wrong.
- Evaluation on compounds already in the public libraries, where the result reflects retrieval. With tandem mass spectra alone, the top-1 rate of automated methods in blind tests is far below the level reached once additional information is allowed.
- A quantitative calibration model that cross-validates well within one batch of samples and fails on another instrument or batch. The held-out part must be formed by instrument or by batch.

## Example: structure elucidation of unknown organic compounds from high-resolution mass spectra and NMR data

Determining the structure of an unknown organic compound from its spectra is everyday work in synthesis and natural-product research, and a problem that has long resisted full automation. The difficulty runs through every stage: mass alone does not determine the molecular formula uniquely (an instrument with 3 ppm mass accuracy and 2% error in isotope abundances outperforms a 0.1 ppm instrument that uses no isotope information); the number of candidate structures under one molecular formula is enormous; and conclusions reached by manual interpretation have been revised before. Blind tests of automated methods show the gap: in CASMI 2016 the best methods using tandem mass spectra alone ranked the correct structure first in over 30% of the challenges, and in about 70% once additional information such as metadata was allowed. The task gives high-resolution mass spectra and 1D and 2D NMR data for a set of tens of compounds of known structure, and asks for each structure to be elucidated and checked back against predicted spectra.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** For each unknown the task statement gives the high-resolution mass spectrum (with isotope peaks and tandem mass spectra) and the raw data and acquisition conditions of the ¹H, ¹³C, DEPT, HSQC, HMBC and COSY spectra, and withholds names, sources and all identifiers; the environment provides fixed versions of a shift prediction program and a structure generator and has no network access. The deliverables are an `answer.json` for each unknown (molecular formula, candidate structures ranked by confidence, a table of shift assignments atom by atom, the candidates that could not be distinguished and the reasons) and a short report. The format of `answer.json` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by structural content, for illustration only:
  1. Molecular formula and degree of unsaturation: the formula determined from the exact mass and the isotope pattern.
  2. Fragments and functional groups: the type of each carbon and the number of attached hydrogens from ¹³C, DEPT and HSQC, and the functional groups from diagnostic shifts.
  3. Skeleton connectivity: the fragments joined into a complete planar structure through COSY and HMBC correlations.
  4. Ranking and back-check of candidates: the predicted spectra of all candidates compared with the measured spectra, with the grounds on which the first choice is preferred to the others.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the isotope pattern computed from the candidate formula, and the shifts and cross peaks predicted from the candidate structure, with residuals against the measurement at the level of the noise and the prediction error) and as text (the peaks and cross peaks relied on, which must actually be present in the spectra; an argument on whether the data determine this part uniquely, and all compatible candidates where they do not). The physical constraints (valence rules and the degree of unsaturation) are checked by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge confirms that the cited peaks are present in the data; recomputes the exact mass and isotope pattern from the submitted formula; recomputes the ¹³C shifts of the first-choice structure with the fixed prediction program, whose mean deviation must lie within the error of that program; and then checks against the true structures one by one: an identical planar structure earns the full score, and a true structure inside the candidate set but not ranked first earns part of it. The tolerance is set from the prediction error measured in step 2 on known structures in the same batch of instrument data. Whether stereochemistry is scored is for the expert to decide.
- **Tiers and scoring.** Each unknown is scored separately and the scores are averaged; within one unknown, the molecular formula, the fragments, the planar structure and the back-check are scored in turn, with weights set by the expert. The unknowns are grouped by difficulty (molecular mass, number of heteroatoms, hydrogen-to-carbon ratio), and answering only the easier group correctly still earns the corresponding score.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); a structure given with no assignment table and no back-check, or a structure that disagrees with the peaks it cites (a shortcut; the corresponding items score 0); a submission with the correct formula and fragments but a wrong skeleton connectivity (partially correct); an isomer compatible with the 1D data but contradicted by the HMBC correlations (a wrong solution consistent with part of the data); the expert's manual interpretation (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change the type of data (retrieving structures from a candidate library with tandem mass spectra alone), or turn the task into a form that needs a new measurement: the expert's laboratory acquires the spectra of a new compound within six hours (physical-world observation level 2), and that part is recorded faithfully as not run, as `references/2-research.md` prescribes.
