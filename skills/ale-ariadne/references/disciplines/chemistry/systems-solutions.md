# Chemistry: systems and solutions

This file adds content specific to systems and solutions to `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: chemistry in complex systems: gas-phase and multiphase reactions in the atmosphere and the formation of secondary organic aerosol, the transformation and degradation of pollutants in water and soil, reactions at interfaces such as the air–water interface, and the thermodynamics of solutions and electrolytes (solvation, acid–base and complexation equilibria, activity). For the rate theory of single elementary reactions and combustion kinetics see `structure-dynamics.md`; for catalytic reactions on solid surfaces see `catalysis.md`; for the development and benchmarking of solvation and free-energy methods as such see `theory-computation.md`; for transport in the atmosphere and in water bodies and for regional and global models see `references/disciplines/earth.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: questions in which the mechanism can be written as a list of reactions and computed with a box model, and chamber experiments or evaluated rate constants exist to compare against. An example is the regeneration of OH radicals in the photooxidation of isoprene at low NO: one box-model run takes only minutes, the experimental data of the SAPHIR chamber are public, and the rates of the key unimolecular reactions have been measured in the laboratory.
- Build with priority: thermodynamic quantities of solutions for which experimental values exist to compare against, for example hydration free energies, pKa values, partition coefficients and activity coefficients of electrolytes. The field tests predictions by blind challenges (the SAMPL series) and public databases (FreeSolv), and scoring on held-out compounds is possible.
- Defer: the yield of secondary organic aerosol and particle-phase chemistry. Gas-phase elementary reactions can be computed, but the reaction network is vast, particle-phase processes are hard to treat, and the yields measured in chambers are affected by wall losses (the loss of vapors to the walls can cause formation to be underestimated by a factor of up to about 4). Discuss with the expert a formulation that fixes the gas-phase mechanism and the gas–particle partitioning model in advance and asks only for conclusions within the model, or one that compares the relative ordering of yields across precursors and NOx levels.
- Defer: the degradation rates and pathways of persistent pollutants such as PFAS in real aqueous environments. Bond dissociation energies and the barriers of the steps can be computed, but the effects of solvent, pH, coexisting ions and reactive species are hard to include. The task can instead fix the solvent model and ask for the relative ordering of the C–F bond dissociation energies and the position where a bond breaks first, compared with the trends of existing structure–reactivity experiments (for example the reductive defluorination of 34 PFAS structures by hydrated electrons).
- Defer: reaction acceleration at the air–water interface and in microdroplets. It needs molecular dynamics of the interface, and the experimental conclusion itself is still disputed: the reported concentration of H₂O₂ formed spontaneously in water microdroplets fell from about 30 µM to 0.3–1.5 µM under ozone-free conditions, and its origin remains unsettled. The task can instead fix the interface model in advance and ask only for quantities that can be recomputed within the model, for example the free-energy difference of a species between the interface and the bulk.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The version and extent of the reaction mechanism: which mechanism is used (for example which version of the MCM and the subset for which precursors), which reactions may be modified, and which evaluation the rate constants are taken from.
- Experimental conditions and constrained quantities: temperature, pressure, humidity, photolysis frequencies, NO and O₃ levels, dilution and wall losses; which quantities in the box model are constrained to measured values and which are used for comparison.
- Whether the comparison data come from chamber experiments, flow-tube experiments or field observations; the known interferences of the measurement methods and their correction, and the measurement uncertainty.
- The form of the conclusion: concentration time series, yields, radical budgets and regeneration efficiency, or a choice between mechanisms; the tolerance is set from the measurement uncertainty and the evaluated uncertainty of the rate constants.
- For solution tasks, the standard state, concentration scale and reference temperature; how ionic strength, pH and ion pairing are treated; which thermodynamic database is used and whether it is internally consistent.
- The treatment of the solvent: implicit, explicit or a mixture of the two; how conformers, protonation states and tautomers are chosen; whether the field accepts this setting.
- What "degradation" means in an environmental-fate task: disappearance of the parent compound, the extent of defluorination or complete mineralization; whether transformation products must be identified.
- How far the conclusion may be extrapolated from laboratory conditions to the real environment, as stated by the expert.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Rerunning the mechanism | The agent judge runs the submitted mechanism file in a fixed box model with the experimental constraints, and for the experiments given in the task statement and the held-out experiments computes the ratio of modeled to measured values of the target species, which must lie within an interval set from the measurement uncertainty | Uses existing data, must pass |
| Consistency across several observables | One mechanism must reproduce the time series of the radicals, of the decay of the precursor and of the main products together; a mechanism that brings one quantity into agreement while the others deviate does not score | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Ablation and sensitivity analysis of reactions | The reactions the submitter claims to be decisive are removed one at a time and the model is rerun; the target quantity must change by the stated amount. The agent judge also varies the rate constants within their evaluated uncertainty and checks that the conclusion is unchanged | Pure computation, counts for part of the score |
| Soundness of the mechanism | Every reaction conserves elements and charge; rate constants differ from evaluated data or laboratory measurements by no more than their uncertainty, and added reactions come with a source or a computed basis; the mechanism contains no unspecified species | Must pass |
| Held-out solution thermodynamic quantities | Predicted hydration free energies, pKa values, partition coefficients or activity coefficients are compared with held-out experimental values; the root-mean-square error and the rank correlation are reported and compared with the experimental uncertainty and a baseline method | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Rerunning an equilibrium calculation | With the thermodynamic database fixed in the task statement, the agent judge recomputes the speciation, saturation indices or pH, and checks mass balance and charge balance | Pure computation |
| Relative ordering of degradation sites | With the settings fixed in the task statement, the agent judge recomputes bond dissociation energies or barriers on the submitted structures, checks that the ordering of the sites agrees with the reference computation, and compares it with the experimental structure–reactivity trends | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2 |

## Sources of existing data

- Gas-phase kinetics: the IUPAC evaluation of kinetic data for atmospheric chemistry and the NASA JPL data evaluation (recommended rate constants with their uncertainties), and the NIST Chemical Kinetics Database; the Master Chemical Mechanism (v3.3.1 covers the degradation of methane and 142 non-methane volatile organic compounds, with 5,832 species and 17,224 reactions, and exports to the FACSIMILE and KPP formats). A mechanism is the result of evaluation and estimation, not observed data.
- Chamber experiments: the Database of Atmospheric Simulation Chamber Studies on the EUROCHAMP data portal (now part of ACTRIS), with open access; the ICARUS database in the United States, whose download conditions should be checked before use.
- Solution thermodynamics: FreeSolv (experimental and calculated hydration free energies of several hundred neutral small molecules, CC-BY 4.0 license), the Minnesota Solvation Database (3,037 experimental solvation and transfer free energies for 790 solutes in 92 solvents; free for non-profit users, with a license required for commercial use), and the data released by past SAMPL blind challenges.
- Aqueous reactions and environmental fate: the NDRL/NIST Solution Kinetics Database (rate constants of solution-phase reactions); the EPA CompTox Chemicals Dashboard (lists of compounds and their properties, in which predicted values are computed results, not existing data).
- Equilibrium constants: the thermodynamic databases distributed with PHREEQC. One system can give different results under different databases, so the task statement must specify one.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The answers to mechanism tasks are mostly published and have entered newer versions of the public mechanisms; MCM v3.3.1, for example, already contains the isomerization reactions of isoprene peroxy radicals. The base mechanism given in the task statement must state its version and have the relevant reactions removed; scoring rests on rerunning the submitted mechanism, not on conclusions in words.
- Chamber data portals publish the time series of every species. Hold out some of the experiments (for example one NO level); their observables are kept out of the environment and are used for checking during review.
- The experimental values of FreeSolv and of past SAMPL challenges can be downloaded and have been widely used for parametrization and model training. Use measurements published after a split in time, or data the expert has not yet published.
- For tasks whose reference is a computed value (bond dissociation energies, barriers), make the submitted structures the object of scoring, recomputed by the agent judge; the task statement gives no literature values.
- The task statement contains no experiment numbers, data-portal entry IDs or paper titles.

## Tools and compute

- Box models: AtChem2 (MIT license, reads mechanisms in the FACSIMILE format), PyCHAM (GPL-3.0 license, with gas–particle partitioning and wall losses), KPP, Cantera (BSD license); the code of F0AM is under the GPL-3.0 license but has to run in MATLAB.
- Mechanism generation and property estimation: GECKO-A (a generator of explicit mechanisms; it is not released publicly and is obtained from its developers only for collaborative projects), UManSysProp (estimates properties such as vapor pressure from structure, GPL-3.0 license), AIOMFAC (an activity coefficient model).
- Solution equilibria and electrolytes: PHREEQC (released by the United States Geological Survey), E-AIM.
- Molecular-level calculation: xtb and CREST, Psi4, ORCA (implicit solvent); CP2K, GROMACS, OpenMM (molecular dynamics with explicit solvent and of interfaces). COSMOtherm, the usual implementation of COSMO-RS, is commercial software.
- Compute: one box-model run takes seconds to minutes on an ordinary computer, and solving one mechanism task takes tens to hundreds of runs; the reruns and ablations during review number in the tens, the same order of magnitude. First-principles molecular dynamics with explicit solvent or of an interface needs several days or more on a CPU cluster. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- A mechanism tested directly against field observations. Field measurements of OH have suffered from interference: in a side-by-side measurement in a California forest, the chemical-removal method gave only 40%–60% of the OH given by the traditional method and agreed better with the model.
- An unspecified species or an artificial regeneration term added to bring the model into agreement with the observations. The literature has used this to quantify the gap in the models, but it does not constitute a mechanism.
- Only one species compared. Adjusting rate constants until OH agrees may leave the product yields off; several observables must be checked together.
- A tolerance set tighter than the measurement uncertainty or the evaluated uncertainty of the rate constants.
- Processes specific to the chamber (wall losses, dilution, trace species released from the walls) left out of the model, with the resulting deviation attributed to the chemical mechanism.
- Conclusions reached under chamber conditions extrapolated directly to the real atmosphere. NO levels, light and the lifetimes of peroxy radicals differ, and the dominant channel changes with them.
- Solvation free energies and pKa values of charged species computed directly with an implicit solvent, where the error is far larger than for neutral molecules. A thermodynamic cycle, a reference acid or a correction with explicit water molecules is needed, and the procedure must be fixed in the task statement.
- The result of an equilibrium calculation depends on the thermodynamic database chosen, and the difference between databases is taken for an error of the solver, or ignored.
- The degradation pathway in an aqueous environment inferred directly from the size of gas-phase bond dissociation energies. The actual initial step may be electron transfer or decarboxylation instead of homolysis.

## Example: regeneration of OH radicals in the oxidation of isoprene at low NO

Isoprene is the non-methane volatile organic compound with the largest emissions. In the traditional mechanism the regeneration of OH depends on the reaction of HO₂ with NO, so isoprene should consume OH heavily at low NO; field observations nevertheless show OH far above the models (in the observations in the Pearl River Delta, model and observation agree above 1 ppb NO, and the model is too low by a factor of 8 at 0.02 ppb NO). Peeters and co-workers proposed from quantum chemical calculations that isoprene peroxy radicals isomerize by a 1,6-hydrogen shift and that the products react further to regenerate OH. Experiments in the SAPHIR chamber (NO between 0.15 and 2 ppbv) support this channel: at the lowest NO the regeneration efficiency of OH is about 0.5, a factor of 2–3 above what is possible without unimolecular reactions; MCM v3.3.1 still underestimates OH by about 30%, while a mechanism in which at least half of the peroxy radicals are lost through isomerization at low NO agrees with the measurement (ratio of model to measurement 0.97 ± 0.10). The task asks the solving agent to start from a base mechanism without isomerization reactions and to arrive independently at a mechanism that explains these experiments.

The following is illustrated for the Explanation class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives, for several chamber experiments, the constraints (temperature, photolysis frequencies, NO, O₃, the amounts of isoprene injected, the dilution rate) and the observables (time series of OH, HO₂, OH reactivity, isoprene and the main products), together with a base mechanism of stated version from which the isomerization reactions have been removed and a fixed box model; several further experiments at different NO levels are held out, and only their constraints are given. The deliverables are a mechanism file, simulated time series for each experiment, a table of the OH budget and a short report. The format of the mechanism file and of the time series is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Isomerization of the peroxy radicals: which isomers undergo the 1,6-hydrogen shift, and how the competition between its rate and the reactions of the peroxy radicals with NO and HO₂ changes with NO.
  2. Fate of the isomerization products: how much OH and HO₂ the hydroperoxy aldehydes and other products return through photolysis and subsequent reactions.
  3. Control and extrapolation: at high NO the mechanism reduces to the traditional chemistry; predictions are given for the held-out NO levels, with an account of why the regeneration efficiency in the chamber is still below the value needed to explain the field observations.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the phenomenon (the change of the regeneration efficiency with NO, the time series of the products). The deliverable is the mechanism, as computation (the box model reproduces the phenomenon without OH being constrained; with the relevant reactions removed the regeneration efficiency falls, which is the ablation) and as mathematics (expressions for the branching ratios of the loss channels and the OH budget equation, in which each rate constant corresponds to an entry in the mechanism file).
- **Checks.** The agent judge reruns the submitted mechanism in the fixed box model: the ratio of modeled to measured OH must lie within a provisional 0.8–1.2 both on the experiments given in the task statement and on the held-out experiments, with the sum of the products and the OH reactivity matching as well; with the added reactions removed, the regeneration efficiency at the lowest NO must fall clearly; the rate constants of the 1,6-hydrogen shift must agree within their uncertainty with the laboratory values (3.6 s⁻¹ and 0.4 s⁻¹ at 298 K for the two kinds of isomer), or a computed basis must be given; every reaction conserves elements. The tolerances are provisional and are set from the uncertainty of the OH measurement and the spread between experiments observed in trial runs.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); OH constrained to the measured values, or an unspecified species added that converts HO₂ directly into OH (a shortcut; the corresponding items score 0); the base mechanism without isomerization reactions (the baseline); the isoprene mechanism of MCM v3.3.1 submitted as it stands (partially correct: OH is still underestimated by about 30% and the sum of the products is overestimated); a mechanism matching the best fit in the literature (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change the precursor (ozonolysis of monoterpenes) or the system (the reaction network of the oxidation of pollutants by OH radicals in water); the workflow and the checks stay the same.
