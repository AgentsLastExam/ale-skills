# Chemistry: structure and dynamics

This file adds content specific to structure and dynamics to `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: reaction kinetics and rate theory in the gas and condensed phases (transition-state theory, tunneling, pressure dependence), chemical kinetic mechanisms of combustion and plasmas, the calculation and assignment of rotational, vibrational and electronic spectra, molecular reaction dynamics and ultrafast nonadiabatic dynamics. For the microkinetics of surface reactions see `catalysis.md`; for the mechanisms and selectivity of organic reactions see `mechanism-properties.md`; for reaction networks in the atmosphere and in water see `systems-solutions.md`; for questions whose purpose is quantitative analysis from spectra or the structure elucidation of unknowns see `measurement-imaging.md`; for the fitting of potential-energy surfaces and the development of dynamics methods themselves see `theory-computation.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: rate constants and tunneling in gas-phase elementary reactions of small molecules, for example k(T) over a given temperature range for reactions such as H + O₂ or OH + H₂. The workflow of a high-accuracy potential-energy surface followed by transition-state theory is mature, and experimental rate constants are accurate and collected in evaluations, which makes a tolerance easy to state.
- Build with priority: the assignment of vibrational and electronic spectra, for example assigning the fundamentals, overtones and combination bands of a small molecule by anharmonic calculation, or telling conformers apart by their rotational constants. Computed and experimental spectra can be compared peak by peak.
- Build with priority: assembling a mechanism from elementary-reaction data and predicting macroscopic observables, for example the ignition delay and laminar flame speed of hydrogen or syngas. Zero- and one-dimensional simulations finish in minutes on an ordinary computer, and shock-tube and rapid-compression-machine data are public and plentiful.
- Defer: ultrafast nonadiabatic dynamics in the condensed phase, for example the quantum yield and time constants of a photoisomerization in solution. The methods are still developing, the cost is high and benchmarks are few. Discuss with the expert a formulation that moves to a small gas-phase molecule, fixes the electronic-structure method and initial conditions in advance, and compares with a published ultrafast diffraction or transient-spectroscopy signal; the prediction challenge on cyclobutanone excited at 200 nm took this form, with more than ten simulation papers submitted before the results of the ultrafast electron diffraction experiment were released.
- Defer: questions for which a new rate measurement or a new spectroscopic measurement is the only criterion and cannot be completed within six hours; plasma-chemistry systems whose electron-impact cross sections are missing or contradictory, where the conclusion depends on the choice of cross-section data and the question can be recast by fixing the cross-section set in advance and asking only for conclusions within the model.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The operational definition of the target observable: whether ignition delay is taken at the steep pressure rise, at the maximum OH concentration or at the maximum of its rate of change; whether a rate constant is the high-pressure limit, the value at a given pressure or an overall value; whether a spectral peak position is the band center or the band head.
- The range of conditions: temperature, pressure, equivalence ratio and diluent gas; for spectra the temperature, phase and resolution.
- The source and uncertainty of the experimental data: shock tube, rapid compression machine or flow reactor; the error of each data point; whether facility-dependent non-ideal effects (rate of pressure rise, heat loss) are on record.
- The level of the model: the reactor model (constant-volume adiabatic, or a prescribed pressure or volume history); the rate theory (conventional or variational transition-state theory, tunneling model, master equation); the electronic-structure method and the anharmonic treatment.
- The source of the thermochemical data and its consistency with the rate constants (reverse rates obtained from equilibrium constants).
- The evaluated uncertainty of the rate constant of each elementary reaction, and the range within which it may be adjusted.
- The form of the conclusion: a curve, the assignment of a set of peaks, the identification of the controlling reactions, or a choice between mechanisms; what the tolerance rests on (experimental scatter, method error).

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Resimulation from the mechanism file | The agent judge reruns the submitted mechanism file under held-out conditions to obtain ignition delays or flame speeds and does not use the values the submitter reports; the error function is the mean square of the deviation in ln τ divided by the experimental standard deviation | Uses existing data, must pass |
| Validity of the mechanism | Elements and charge are conserved in every reaction; reverse rates follow from the thermochemical data; every rate constant lies within its evaluated uncertainty range, and bimolecular rate constants do not exceed the collision limit | Must pass |
| Sensitivity and controlling reactions | With the submitted mechanism, the agent judge recomputes the sensitivity coefficients of the ignition delay to each rate constant; the top-ranked reactions agree with the submitted conclusion | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2 |
| Comparison of rate constants with experiment | Over the given temperature range, the ratio of the submitted k(T) to the evaluated value or to held-out measurements lies within a tolerance factor that is stated as a function of temperature | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Recomputation of stationary points and rates | Energies and frequencies of the submitted reactants and transition state are recomputed at the fixed level, and from them the agent judge recomputes the transition-state-theory rate constant and the tunneling correction, which agree with the values the submitter reports | Pure computation |
| Peak-by-peak comparison of spectra | The submitted assignment table is compared with the experimental peak positions one by one: the deviations in position are within tolerance, the order of relative intensities agrees, and the direction and size of the shifts on isotopic substitution agree | Uses existing data |
| Dynamical observables | For nonadiabatic dynamics tasks: the populations as functions of time or the diffraction signal are recomputed from the submitted ensemble of trajectories and compared with the reference computation or the experimental signal, and the statistical error from the number of trajectories is checked | Pure computation, or uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |

## Sources of existing data

- Combustion experiments: ReSpecTh (indirect measurements such as ignition delays and flame speeds, and directly measured gas-phase rate constants, in XML format, free registration required); ignition data organized in the ChemKED format; tables in the original papers.
- Rate constants and thermochemistry: the NIST Chemical Kinetics Database; the evaluated kinetic data for combustion of Baulch and co-workers, which state an uncertainty for each reaction; ATcT and Burcat's thermochemical database.
- Spectra: HITRAN (high-resolution gas-phase line parameters, registration required); NIST Chemistry WebBook and CCCBDB (experimental vibrational frequencies and rotational constants); the CDMS and JPL molecular line catalogs (rotational spectra). The high-temperature line lists of ExoMol are mainly computed, so they count as pure computation, not existing data.
- Plasmas: LXCat (electron-impact cross sections and transport coefficients). Cross-section sets from different sources can differ markedly for the same process.

## Preventing answer leakage

- Published mechanism files can be downloaded directly, and simulation software ships with example mechanisms; scoring on agreement with experiment alone can be passed by downloading. Treat the result of running a published mechanism as a known result with an expected score given by the expert; the rest of the score comes from identifying the controlling reactions, the sensitivity analysis and the prediction for held-out conditions.
- Experimental ignition delays and rate constants are all published. The experimental values for the held-out conditions are kept out of the task statement, or a dataset published after a split in time is used.
- For spectral assignment tasks: assignment tables for common molecules are in NIST and HITRAN. Switch to isotopologues, to conformers or clusters that the databases do not contain, with reference values from high-accuracy computation or from spectra that have not been published.
- The task statement contains no names or authors of mechanisms, no dataset IDs and no paper titles; the agent judge reruns the simulation and does not compare against curves the submitter reports.

## Tools and compute

- Chemical kinetics simulation: Cantera (open source; zero-dimensional reactors, one-dimensional flames and sensitivity analysis); Chemkin needs a commercial license.
- Rate theory and master equation: Arkane (distributed with RMG-Py), MESS, MultiWell, Polyrate (Apache 2.0 license).
- Spectrum calculation: anharmonic vibrations (VPT2) are implemented in ORCA, CFOUR and Gaussian, none of which is open source and each of which has its own license terms; PGOPHER simulates and fits rotational spectra.
- Nonadiabatic dynamics: SHARC and Newton-X (surface hopping), MCTDH (quantum dynamics).
- Plasma kinetics: BOLSIG+ (solver of the electron Boltzmann equation), ZDPlasKin.
- Compute: one zero-dimensional ignition simulation takes seconds, and tens of conditions together with the sensitivity analysis take minutes to tens of minutes on an ordinary computer; a high-accuracy potential-energy surface and anharmonic calculation for a small molecule take tens to hundreds of CPU core-hours; an ensemble of nonadiabatic trajectories costs several orders of magnitude more. The resimulation during review is comparable in size to solving. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- A definition of ignition delay that differs from the experiment. The steep pressure rise, the maximum OH concentration and the maximum of d[OH]/dt give different values, so simulation and experiment are not comparing the same quantity.
- Low-temperature shock-tube data simulated with a constant-volume adiabatic model. Behind the reflected shock the pressure rises at a few percent per millisecond, and at the low-temperature conditions with long ignition delays (below about 1000 K) the measured values can be several times shorter than the ideal model predicts; simulate with the prescribed pressure history, or exclude such data.
- The effect of impurities ignored. Simulations show that H atoms at the ppb level in the initial mixture shorten the ignition delay above 1000 K, by up to about 20%.
- A tolerance on ignition delay set as a linear deviation. The experimental scatter is proportional to τ; the error belongs on ln τ, normalized by the experimental standard deviation.
- Rate constants adjusted outside their evaluated uncertainty range in order to match experiment, or only the forward reaction adjusted without keeping consistency with the thermochemistry.
- Pressure dependence and third-body efficiencies handled incorrectly. H + O₂ (+M) → HO₂ (+M) is sensitive to the kind of diluent gas, and data diluted in Ar and in N₂ cannot share one set of parameters without distinction.
- A tolerance on rate constants not adjusted with temperature. An error of 1 kcal/mol in the barrier amounts to a factor of 5.4 at 298 K and only 1.65 at 1000 K.
- Harmonic frequencies compared with experimental fundamentals without scaling; or an assignment made on peak positions alone, without checking intensities, isotope shifts and symmetry.
- The Wigner tunneling correction used for a hydrogen-transfer reaction at low temperature; or the contributions of multiple conformers and hindered internal rotation to the partition function ignored.

## Example: ignition delay of a hydrogen combustion mechanism

The hydrogen–oxygen system is the foundation of the combustion mechanisms of all fuels: 8 species (H₂, O₂, H, O, OH, HO₂, H₂O₂, H₂O) and about 20 reversible elementary reactions describe it, and the 2012 mechanism of Burke and co-workers has 19 reactions. At high temperature ignition is controlled by the chain-branching reaction H + O₂ → O + OH; as pressure rises and temperature falls, H + O₂ (+M) → HO₂ (+M) competes with it, and ignition comes under the control of the reactions of HO₂ and H₂O₂ (H₂ + HO₂ → H₂O₂ + H, H₂O₂ (+M) → 2OH (+M)). In 2014 Olm and co-workers compared 19 published mechanisms against experimental data including 770 shock-tube ignition-delay data points (53 datasets), and the shock-tube data below 1000 K were not well predicted. The task asks the solving agent to assemble a mechanism from elementary-reaction data, predict the ignition delay over a range of temperature and pressure, and name the controlling reactions in each regime.

The following is illustrated for the Prediction class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives the thermochemical data, candidate rate expressions for each elementary reaction with their evaluated uncertainties, part of the shock-tube ignition-delay data (mixture composition, temperature, pressure, ignition criterion and error) and the simulation environment; it provides no complete published mechanism file, and the experimental values for the held-out conditions are kept only in `tests/`. The deliverables are a mechanism file, a table of ignition delays at the specified conditions, the results of the sensitivity analysis and a short report. The format of the mechanism file and of the ignition-delay table is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. The high-temperature chain-branching regime (above about 1000 K): the choice of rates for H + O₂ → O + OH and the other chain-branching and chain-propagating reactions, and the variation of ignition delay with temperature.
  2. Pressure dependence and the turnover: the pressure dependence and third-body efficiencies of H + O₂ (+M) → HO₂ (+M), and the shift with pressure of the turnover temperature at which chain branching and HO₂ formation balance.
  3. The high-pressure, low-to-intermediate-temperature regime: the formation and consumption of HO₂ and H₂O₂, and the control of ignition by H₂O₂ decomposition; for shock-tube data with a pressure rise, a reactor model with the prescribed pressure history.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the target quantity as defined in the task statement; the true value is not provided to the solving agent. The deliverable is the prediction with its uncertainty, as text (the chosen rate expressions and reactor model, with an argument that they apply in that regime) and as computation (the predicted values, convergence of the ignition delay with respect to the integration tolerances, the sensitivity analysis on each rate constant, which is the ablation of the Prediction class, and an uncertainty estimate for the predicted values). The comparison with experimental ignition delays is made by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge first checks the validity of the mechanism, then reruns the submitted mechanism under the held-out conditions and extracts τ with the same ignition criterion as the experiment. The error function is the mean square of the deviation in ln τ divided by the experimental standard deviation, which is taken to be no less than 10% of τ; an error function of no more than 9 (a root-mean-square deviation within 3 standard deviations) passes, and the score varies continuously with the error function. The most sensitive reactions in each regime must agree with the agent judge's recomputation. The threshold is provisional and is set from the error-function values of published mechanisms on the same data.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); a table of ignition delays with no mechanism file, or values in the table that disagree with the resimulation (a shortcut; the corresponding values score 0); a mechanism whose rate constants were adjusted outside the uncertainty range to fit the data in the task statement (a shortcut; it fails the validity check); a mechanism that agrees at high temperature but lacks the H₂O₂ reactions or does not treat the pressure rise, and is off by a factor of several in the high-pressure, low-to-intermediate-temperature regime (partially correct); a published and widely validated mechanism run as it is (a known result); a simplified mechanism without pressure dependence (a baseline).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change the fuel (syngas, methane, ammonia) or the observable (laminar flame speed, species concentrations in a flow reactor); the workflow and the checks stay the same.
