# Medicine: pathology and tissue diagnosis

This file adds content specific to pathology and tissue diagnosis to `references/disciplines/medicine.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: histopathology and digital slides (whole-slide imaging and its quality control, computational pathology models, automated grading and reproducibility), immunohistochemistry and molecular pathology (marker panels and scoring, in situ hybridization and detection of fusion genes), tumor classification and staging (stage, margin and nodal assessment, molecular subtyping of tumors, tumor microenvironment and immune infiltrate), spatial omics on tissue (spatial transcriptomics in clinical samples, multiplexed protein imaging), and death certification and mortality coding (selection and coding of the underlying cause of death). For radiological imaging see `medical-imaging.md`; for markers in blood and body fluids see `laboratory-medicine.md`; for the clinical interpretation of tumor sequencing variants see `precision-medicine.md`; for estimating disease burden from cause-of-death data see `global-environmental-health.md`; for basic research questions in spatial omics see `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: grading and detection questions on public whole-slide images where the reference standard is set by specialist pathologists and the performance of pathologists themselves is available for comparison. An example is the detection of sentinel lymph node metastases of breast cancer: in CAMELYON16 the best algorithm reached a slide-level AUC of 0.994, 11 pathologists reading under a time constraint averaged 0.810, and one pathologist reading without a time constraint reached 0.966.
- Build with priority: questions that predict molecular status from H&E slides and can be re-tested on a cohort from another source. An example is predicting microsatellite instability from colorectal cancer slides: the diagnostic slides and molecular labels of TCGA are publicly available, the slides of CPTAC can serve as the external cohort, and the criterion comes down to the AUROC on the external cohort.
- Build with priority: coding questions governed by written rules. An example is determining the underlying cause of death from the causes entered on the lines of a death certificate under the selection rules of ICD-10, or inferring the cause of death from a verbal autopsy questionnaire: the PHMRC gold standard verbal autopsy data are public, and the criterion comes down to agreement with the reference coding and the accuracy of cause-specific mortality fractions at population level.
- Defer: readings for which the reference standard itself has very low agreement between pathologists. An example is the diagnosis of atypical hyperplasia on breast biopsy: in a study with 115 participating pathologists, concordance with the consensus reference diagnosis was only 48% for this category, against 75.3% over all cases. Discuss with the expert a formulation that takes the distribution of scores from several pathologists as the reference, or that switches to objective sub-tasks such as nucleus detection and counting of positive cells.
- Defer: questions that can be judged only by new sectioning, staining or sequencing, for example validating a new antibody clone or a new spatial transcriptomics experiment; and questions that can be judged only by a prospective reader study, for example whether diagnostic assistance improves the diagnoses of pathologists. All such verification takes more than six hours.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- Specimen and slide preparation: organ and specimen type (biopsy or resection, frozen or paraffin-embedded), stain, scanner and magnification (microns per pixel), file format.
- Where the reference standard comes from: routine pathology reports, a single specialist pathologist, a panel consensus, or a molecular assay or follow-up outcome; the agreement within the reference standard itself (for example the weighted kappa between experts).
- The grading or scoring system and its version: Gleason score and ISUP grade group, Nottingham grade, the edition of TNM staging, the scoring rules for HER2 and PD-L1 and the antibody clone used.
- The unit of analysis and the level of aggregation: tile, slide, block, case or patient; how several slides of one patient are aggregated into a patient-level conclusion.
- The origin and splitting of the data: whether development data and held-out data come from different laboratories and scanners; whether public data carry patient identifiers so that they can be split by patient.
- Which pretrained models may be used: whether pathology foundation models are allowed, what their licenses are, and whether their training data overlap with the held-out data.
- For cause-of-death coding tasks: the version of ICD, the version of the selection rules, whether the decision tables of an automated coding system are used, and who produced the reference coding.
- The form of the conclusion and the tolerance: an agreement coefficient (quadratically weighted kappa), AUROC, FROC for lesion detection, or coding agreement; whether the tolerance takes the agreement between pathologists as its ceiling.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/medicine.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Grading agreement with an expert reference standard | The agent judge runs the submitted program, computes the quadratically weighted kappa on held-out slides with a bootstrap confidence interval, and compares it with the kappa of pathologists against the same reference standard | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Slide-level classification and lesion-level detection | AUROC at slide level; at lesion level FROC, that is, the detection rate at given numbers of false positives per slide, with the rule for counting a hit fixed in advance | Uses existing data |
| Generalization to a held-out center | The same model is evaluated again on slides from a laboratory and scanner not used in development, and the drop relative to held-out slides from the development source is reported | Uses existing data, must pass |
| Patient-level aggregation | The agent judge confirms that the split is by patient, aggregates slide-level outputs into a patient-level conclusion by a fixed rule (for example nodal stage), and computes metrics and confidence intervals with the patient as the unit | Must pass |
| Screening for confounding by source | A probe is trained on the model's features with the submitting institution or the scanner as the label; if the source can be told apart and is associated with the outcome, re-evaluation under a split that holds out institutions is required | Uses existing data, counts for part of the score |
| Recount of quantitative scores | Fraction of immunohistochemistry-positive cells, mitotic count or density of infiltrating lymphocytes: compared with held-out manual counts, reporting the Bland–Altman bias and the intraclass correlation coefficient, and compared with the difference between observers | Uses existing data |
| Coding agreement for the underlying cause of death | Compared with the reference coding: agreement at the ICD three-character and chapter levels, chance-corrected concordance by cause, and the accuracy of cause-specific mortality fractions at population level (CSMF accuracy) | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |

## Sources of existing data

- Challenge data: CAMELYON16 and CAMELYON17 (1,399 whole-slide images of lymph nodes in total, from five Dutch medical centers, under a CC0 license, about 3 TB; the test annotations of CAMELYON16 have been released, those of CAMELYON17 have not); PANDA (a development set of 10,616 prostate biopsy slides, released under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license, so noncommercial research use only and derived data must carry the same license; the test set and the external validation sets are not public).
- Cohorts with molecular and clinical labels: the diagnostic slides of TCGA and its clinical and molecular data are open access through the GDC, while individual-level sequencing data are under controlled access; the slides of CPTAC are on TCIA; GTEx provides slides of normal tissue.
- Spatial omics: HEST-1k (1,276 spatial transcriptomics samples with their paired H&E whole-slide images, under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license); the multiplexed imaging data of HTAN and HuBMAP, whose access conditions vary with the data level.
- Observer agreement: the case-by-case scores of reader studies are mostly not public; agreement coefficients reported in the literature depend on the case mix and the reference standard, so check each one before using it as a ceiling.
- Causes of death: the multiple cause-of-death public-use files of the US NCHS and CDC WONDER; the WHO Mortality Database; the PHMRC gold standard verbal autopsy dataset (public through the GHDx).

## Preventing answer leakage

- The test annotations of CAMELYON16 and the results of the teams have been released, and derived datasets such as PatchCamelyon are taken from the same slides. When it is used as the held-out set, the annotations can be had by downloading. Switch to a subset whose annotations are not public, or have the expert annotate unpublished slides separately.
- One patient has several slides, several blocks and several biopsies; the PANDA development set averages about 5 slides per patient. Split by patient; when public data carry no patient identifiers, do not carve a held-out set out of them by a random split.
- TCGA slide identifiers contain the code of the submitting institution, and staining and preparation features are enough to identify the institution, which in turn is associated with outcomes such as molecular subtype and survival. Remove the original identifiers from the task statement and hold out by institution.
- The pretraining data of pathology foundation models may include TCGA, CAMELYON or PANDA. UNI states that it did not use these public collections; other models may differ. Restrict the weights that may be used, or confirm that the held-out data are not in their training sets.
- Leaderboard scores and winning solutions can be found by search, so the metric that counts is the one the agent judge obtains by rerunning the submitted program. File names, the label-area image of a slide and the scan date can leak case identity and class, and must be removed before the data are provided.

## Tools and compute

- Reading and quality control: OpenSlide and Bio-Formats (reading the whole-slide formats of the vendors); HistoQC (slide quality control); QuPath (viewing, annotation and cell detection, GPL license).
- Model building: CLAM, TIAToolbox and Slideflow (tile extraction and multiple-instance learning pipelines); Macenko and Vahadane stain normalization; HoVer-Net, Cellpose and StarDist (nucleus segmentation).
- Foundation models: pathology foundation models such as UNI and CONCH require an individual access request, are licensed for noncommercial academic research only and may not be redistributed (UNI is under a CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license), so they cannot be put into a task package.
- Spatial omics and cause-of-death coding: Scanpy, Squidpy and Seurat; the Iris automated coding system; openVA (InterVA and InSilicoVA).
- Compute: one whole-slide image is several GB, and the complete CAMELYON data are about 3 TB; extracting features from ten thousand slides with a pretrained encoder takes tens to hundreds of GPU hours, and training a multiple-instance model on cached features takes hours; the PANDA challenge required an algorithm to process the evaluation set within 6 hours and 16 GB of GPU memory, and review, which runs only inference and metric computation, is of that scale. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- Routine pathology reports or the annotations of a single pathologist taken as noise-free truth. The annotations of the PANDA development set come from routine reports and from a single specialist, whereas the reference standards of the internal validation set and of the US external validation set were set by panels of specialists, and several winning teams used label denoising in training.
- A target or tolerance set above the agreement between pathologists. In PANDA the pairwise quadratically weighted kappa between the specialists who contributed to the reference standards was 0.907 and 0.926; metric differences above that level are hard to interpret.
- A random split by tile, which puts neighboring tiles of one slide on both sides.
- Evaluation only on slides from the same source as the development data. The selected PANDA algorithms had a quadratically weighted kappa of 0.931 on the internal validation set, which fell to 0.862 and 0.868 on the US and European external validation sets.
- A model that has learned the source and not the morphology: stain intensity, scanner, pen marks and the amount of tissue may all be associated with the class.
- A slide-level metric standing in for a patient-level conclusion. A pathologist reaches a diagnosis from all the slides of a patient, and stage is assigned per patient.
- Accuracy and predictive values obtained on a test set with an enriched class mix extrapolated directly to the clinic; kappa depends on the class distribution, and kappa values on different datasets cannot be compared directly.
- Attention heatmaps of multiple-instance learning taken as evidence of mechanism, with no ablation that masks the high-attention regions and checks whether the prediction changes.
- Expression prediction in spatial transcriptomics evaluated with a random split of spots within one slide. Spatial autocorrelation inflates the metrics; hold out by slide or by patient.

## Example: cross-site generalization of Gleason grading on prostate biopsies

Gleason grading is the main basis for prognosis and treatment decisions in prostate cancer, yet agreement between pathologists is limited. The PANDA challenge (2020, with 1,290 developers taking part) released 10,616 whole-slide images of needle biopsies from 2,113 patients of Radboud University Medical Center in the Netherlands and Karolinska Institutet in Sweden, with the ISUP grade group as the label and the quadratically weighted kappa as the metric. The selected algorithms reached a kappa of 0.931 against the reference standard of specialist pathologists on the internal validation set, and 0.862 and 0.868 on the external validation sets from the United States (741 cases) and Europe (330 cases); on a 70-case subset of the internal validation set the algorithms reached 0.876 and 13 general pathologists 0.765. The annotations of the development set come from routine pathology reports (the Dutch part) and from a single specialist (the Swedish part), and are not of the same kind as the reference standards of the validation sets. The task asks what agreement a grading pipeline can reach on slides from a laboratory it has not seen, under such label noise and differences between centers, and how much each improvement contributes.

The following is illustrated for the Development class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives the metric (the quadratically weighted kappa against the specialists' reference standard on held-out slides), the baseline pipeline and its score, the constraints to be kept (the split is by patient; inference finishes within a set time and GPU memory; no pretrained weights other than those listed in the task statement are used; sensitivity for tumor detection is not below the baseline) and the development data. The held-out slides come from a laboratory that took no part in development, and their annotations are kept in the agent judge's material only. The deliverables are a runnable inference program with weights, a prediction file covering every slide, the results of each ablation and a short report. The format of the prediction file (slide identifier and ISUP grade group) is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Label noise: the two sources differ in annotation quality; how annotations that may be wrong are handled in training.
  2. Differences between centers: how differences in staining and scanner are handled so that the model keeps its agreement in a laboratory it has not seen.
  3. Aggregation from tiles to the slide: how the slide-level grade is obtained from tile features while keeping the ordering of the grades.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the part of the existing method the sub-question concerns, with the metric and the constraints to be maintained. The deliverable is the improvement and its contribution to the metric, as text (why the improvement raises kappa on the held-out center, and why the constraints still hold) and as computation (the improved model and its metric; the re-evaluation with the improvement removed, which is the ablation and establishes its contribution to the overall gain). The agent judge reruns inference and recomputes the metric, checking each constraint at the same time.
- **Checks.** The agent judge runs the submitted program on the held-out slides and computes the quadratically weighted kappa with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval (resampling patients), without using the values the submitter reports; it also computes the same metric on held-out slides from the same source as the development data and reports the difference between the two. A gain over the baseline counts only when it lies outside the confidence interval; the score follows the margin above the baseline, with the agreement between specialists as the reference ceiling. The tolerance is set from the variation observed under resampling.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); the baseline pipeline given in the task statement (the baseline); a model not split by patient and tuned on same-source data, which scores high on same-source slides and drops on the held-out center (works only on the instances used in development); a model whose kappa exceeds the baseline but whose inference exceeds the time limit, or whose sensitivity for tumor detection is below the baseline (violates a constraint); public labels found by search and written into the predictions file by file (a shortcut, which fails when the held-out annotations are unpublished); a submission that completes only one improvement, with its ablation (partially correct).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change the organ and grading system (Nottingham grading of breast cancer), or switch to the patient-level nodal staging of CAMELYON17 (also evaluated by the quadratically weighted kappa); the workflow and the checks stay the same.
