# Medicine: health policy and economics

This file adds content specific to health policy and economics to `references/disciplines/medicine.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: payment and insurance design (benefit design and cost sharing, value-based and bundled payment), cost-effectiveness analysis (QALY modeling and utility measurement, budget impact and affordability), health technology assessment (reproducing submission models), equity and access (measuring disparities in access and outcome, social determinants), and regulatory evidence and post-market study. For the design and bias analysis of causal inference from observational data see `epidemiology.md`; for trial design, endpoints and external controls see `clinical-trials.md`; for hospital operations, rostering and quality improvement see `health-systems.md`; for the estimation of disease burden see `global-environmental-health.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: questions that rebuild a decision-analytic model from its published inputs and reproduce its costs, health outcomes, incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) and sensitivity analysis. Examples are the Markov cohort model of combination therapy for HIV and the probabilistic model of total hip replacement prostheses: the inputs are published in full, the modeling workflow is mature, and the agent judge can recompute everything independently and compare it item by item with a reference model.
- Build with priority: questions that reanalyze a classic insurance experiment with public data and an analysis plan written in advance. Examples are the effect of cost sharing on medical spending in the RAND Health Insurance Experiment (whose price elasticity estimate of −0.2 is still in use), and the effect of winning by lottery the chance to apply for Medicaid on health care use, financial strain and health in the Oregon Health Insurance Experiment: random assignment provides identification, and public data and replication code exist to compare against.
- Build with priority: policy quantities that can be recomputed once the inputs are fixed, for example the budget impact under given epidemiological and price inputs, the threshold price at a given willingness-to-pay threshold, or the concentration index of health service use in public survey data. Such results are determined by the model and by arithmetic, not by value judgements.
- Defer: questions in which the real effect of a payment or insurance reform can be judged only after years of observation, for example whether bundled payment or accountable care organizations lower spending without harming quality. The claims data required is under controlled access, and the estimates depend on identifying assumptions. Discuss with the expert building only the sub-problem that can be computed: given a de-identified panel and a difference-in-differences specification fixed in advance, re-estimate the effect and complete the parallel-trends and placebo checks; this sub-problem does not show the effect of the reform in other regions and periods.
- Defer: questions whose inputs are redacted or whose conclusion depends on a value judgement, for example reproducing a health technology assessment submission model that contains confidential discounted prices and unpublished patient-level data, or deciding whether a technology should be reimbursed and what the threshold should be. The former can switch to public list prices and survival data reconstructed from published Kaplan–Meier curves and ask only for conclusions within the model; the latter can become a computation under a given threshold and perspective.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The decision problem: the target population, the full set of strategies compared, the perspective (health system or societal) and the time horizon.
- The reference case followed: the discount rates for costs and health effects, the price year and currency, and how utilities are measured; the rules of the health technology assessment agencies differ between countries.
- The type and structure of the model (decision tree, Markov cohort, partitioned survival, microsimulation, discrete-event simulation), the cycle length and the counting convention within a cycle, and what the structural assumptions rest on.
- Where the effect parameters come from (a single trial, a meta-analysis, an indirect comparison), which distributions are used for survival extrapolation and why, and how long the treatment effect lasts.
- The source of the utility values and the valuation system (the EQ-5D version and the country value set), and the components and sources of the costs.
- How uncertainty is expressed: the parameter ranges of the deterministic sensitivity analysis, the distributions and number of draws of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, and whether the conclusion is given as an ICER, a net monetary benefit or a cost-effectiveness acceptability curve.
- For policy evaluation questions, the identification strategy (random lottery, difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, instrumental variables), the unit of analysis and the level of clustering, and the primary outcomes specified in the pre-analysis plan.
- What the tolerance rests on: rounding of deterministic results, the Monte Carlo error of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, or the deviation the field accepts for a model replication.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/medicine.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Rerun of the submitted model | With the inputs fixed in the task statement, the agent judge reruns the submitted model code; the costs, health outcomes and ICER of each strategy must agree with the values the submitter reports; values that cannot be reproduced score nothing | Pure computation, must pass |
| Comparison with the reference model | Discounted costs, life-years or QALYs, incremental values and the ICER, as well as the state occupancy trace cycle by cycle, agree within tolerance with the reference model built independently in step 2 | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Internal consistency of the model | State occupancies sum to 1 in every cycle; with all utilities set to 1, QALYs equal life-years; with discount rates set to 0, discounted and undiscounted results are identical; with the efficacy of the strategies set equal, the incremental effect is 0 | Must pass |
| Response to changed inputs | The agent judge reruns the submitted model with a set of inputs not given in the task statement (discount rates, time horizon, duration of the treatment effect); the results agree with the reference model within tolerance | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2 |
| Probabilistic sensitivity analysis | Mean costs and effects, the cost-effectiveness acceptability curve and the expected value of perfect information are recomputed from the submitted draws and differ from the reference computation by no more than a few Monte Carlo standard errors | Pure computation, counts for part of the score |
| Ranking of strategies and the efficient frontier | In a comparison of several strategies, the strategies that are dominated or extendedly dominated, the order of the strategies on the frontier and the ICERs between adjacent strategies agree with the reference computation | Pure computation |
| Policy experiment with a lottery or randomized rollout | Given public data and the pre-analysis plan, the intention-to-treat effect, the first stage and the instrumental-variable estimate are re-estimated, with standard errors clustered on the unit of randomization, and agree with the reference analysis within tolerance | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |

## Sources of existing data

- Published decision models: teaching and methodological models whose inputs are published in full (combination therapy for HIV, total hip replacement); the public documents of NICE technology appraisals, in which confidential prices and some data are redacted; the Tufts CEA Registry, which collects published cost-utility analyses and requires a subscription for bulk access. Model results are computed, so they count as pure computation, not existing data.
- Insurance experiments: the public-use data of the Oregon Health Insurance Experiment and the replication code of four papers are provided by NBER, on condition that subjects are not identified, the data is not used commercially and the source is cited; the replication package of the 2013 reanalysis of the RAND Health Insurance Experiment is deposited at openICPSR.
- Spending and use: MEPS (public); the HCUP databases must be purchased and require a signed data use agreement; Medicare claims data requires an application through ResDAC and cannot be redistributed; the public-use files of the Medicare Shared Savings Program released by CMS hold data aggregated at the organization level.
- Costs and utilities: the National Cost Collection of the NHS in England and the unit cost compendium of PSSRU (public); EQ-5D value sets of the various countries are found in the published literature.
- Survival data: Kaplan–Meier curves in trial papers, digitized and reconstructed into approximate individual data with the Guyot algorithm. Reconstructed data is an approximation and is not the same as participant-level data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The ICER and the sensitivity analysis of a published model can be found by searching. Make the submitted model code the object of scoring: the agent judge reruns it, and runs it once more with inputs not given in the task statement.
- Teaching models have complete implementations in the example documentation of several software packages. The task statement changes the discount rates, the time horizon, the duration of the treatment effect or the cost year, so that the public numbers are no longer the answer, or asks for quantities the paper did not report (the trace cycle by cycle, the expected value of perfect information, the threshold price).
- Public trial data comes with official replication code. Ask for an outcome or subgroup that is listed in the pre-analysis plan and covered by neither the papers nor the replication code; the results of the reference analysis are kept only in `tests/`.
- The task statement contains no drug names, model authors, trial names or appraisal document numbers; where needed, the disease states and interventions are rewritten under neutral names.

## Tools and compute

- Decision models: in R, heemod, hesim and rdecision (model building), dampack, BCEA and voi (analysis of results and value of information); TreeAge Pro needs a commercial license; submission models are mostly built in Excel and are rewritten as scripts when rebuilt, so that they can be rerun.
- Survival extrapolation: in R, survival and flexsurv; IPDfromKM (reconstruction of individual data from digitized curves).
- Policy evaluation: in R, fixest (fixed effects, instrumental variables and cluster-robust standard errors) and did (difference-in-differences with multiple periods); Stata needs a commercial license.
- Compute: the deterministic computation of a cohort model takes seconds on one core, and a probabilistic sensitivity analysis of ten thousand draws takes minutes; an individual-level microsimulation needs enough simulated individuals to bring the Monte Carlo error down and can take hours. The rerun during review is of the same order as solving. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- The discounting convention for costs and health effects not fixed. In the model of combination therapy for HIV, the ICER is £6,276 with life-years undiscounted and about £10,200 with life-years discounted at the same 6% as costs.
- The counting convention within a cycle (start of cycle, end of cycle or half-cycle correction) not fixed. The ICER of one model differs by about 1% across the three conventions, so an unstated convention with a tolerance tighter than this leads to wrong verdicts.
- The mean of the ICERs of the individual draws taken as the mean ICER. Average the costs and the effects separately and then divide; where the incremental effect can be negative, use the net monetary benefit instead.
- The ICER computed against the wrong comparator: dominated and extendedly dominated strategies not removed, or a relevant comparator left out.
- Rates and probabilities confused, or transition probabilities not converted when the cycle length is changed.
- The distribution for survival extrapolation chosen on goodness of fit within the trial period alone. Distributions differ enough in the extrapolated part to change the conclusion, and this must be reported as structural uncertainty.
- Too few draws in the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, so that the Monte Carlo error exceeds the difference between strategies; or the transition probabilities out of one state drawn independently, so that they no longer sum to 1, where a Dirichlet distribution should be used.
- When the lottery draws individuals but eligibility extends to the whole household, the probability of selection varies with the number of household members on the list; this variable left uncontrolled, standard errors not clustered by household, or the intention-to-treat estimate stated as the effect of coverage itself. In the Oregon experiment, those selected were only about 25 percentage points more likely to be insured than the controls.
- "Cost-effective at a given threshold" stated as "should be reimbursed". The threshold, the perspective and equity weights are value judgements, and the task does not rule on them.

## Example: rebuilding the cost-effectiveness model of combination therapy for HIV

In 1997 Chancellor and colleagues evaluated the cost-effectiveness of zidovudine plus lamivudine against zidovudine alone for HIV infection with a four-state Markov model: CD4 between 200 and 500, CD4 below 200, AIDS and death, with a cycle of one year. The textbook of Briggs, Claxton and Sculpher gives a simplified version, which has since become a common test case for modeling software: 20 cycles, costs discounted at 6%, life-years undiscounted, and the relative risk of combination therapy, 0.509 (95% confidence interval 0.365–0.710), and the cost of lamivudine both applied in the first two years only; the discounted cost of monotherapy is £44,663 with 7.99 life-years, that of combination therapy £50,602 with 8.94, and the ICER is £6,276 per life-year. The task asks the solving agent to rebuild the model independently from the published inputs and to give the conclusion and its uncertainty under changed settings.

The following is illustrated for the Prediction class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 0.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives the annual transition counts, the direct medical and community care costs of each state, the annual drug costs, the relative risk with its confidence interval, and the counting convention; the discount rates, the time horizon and the duration of the treatment effect take values different from the published version, and no transition probability matrix, result or literature source is given. The deliverables are model code that can be rerun, a `results.json` collecting the discounted costs, life-years, incremental values and ICER of each strategy, the state occupancy trace cycle by cycle, and the draws of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis. The format of `results.json` and of the trace file is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Disease progression: from the transition counts, the transition probabilities under monotherapy, the state occupancy trace and the expected life-years.
  2. Treatment effect: which transitions the relative risk acts on and for how long, and the incremental life-years of combination therapy.
  3. Costs and value: the discounted costs of each strategy, the ICER, and at a given willingness-to-pay threshold the net monetary benefit and the probability that combination therapy is cost-effective.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the target quantity as defined in the task statement; the true value is not provided to the solving agent. The deliverable is the prediction with its uncertainty, as text (the state structure, how the relative risk acts, the discounting and counting conventions, with an argument that they apply) and as computation (the numerical results, convergence in the cycle length and the counting convention, sensitivity analysis on the relative risk, the duration of the treatment effect and the discount rates, which is ablation in the Prediction class, and the uncertainty interval given by the probabilistic sensitivity analysis). The comparison with the reference model is made by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge reruns the submitted code and checks the values the submitter reports; it then runs the code with another set of inputs not given in the task statement and compares with the reference model: deterministic results deviate by no more than 0.5% in relative terms, the state occupancies in each cycle by no more than 0.001 in absolute terms, and the mean costs and effects of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis by no more than 3 Monte Carlo standard errors from the reference computation; the internal consistency checks must pass. The tolerances are provisional: with inputs and conventions fixed, a deterministic model should reproduce exactly, yet one public independent implementation obtains £6,264 against the £6,276 of the textbook, a difference of 0.2%, so a margin is left for rounding and implementation detail.
- **Tiers and scoring.** The base results under the settings of the task statement form the first tier, the response to changed inputs the second, and the probabilistic sensitivity analysis the third; completing only the first tier earns the corresponding score. The weights are set by the expert.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); the published £6,276 copied without a runnable model, or submitted code whose results do not change when the inputs are changed (a shortcut; the corresponding values score 0); a submission with correct base results that applies the treatment effect over the whole time horizon or lacks the probabilistic sensitivity analysis (partially correct); the reference model itself (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Switch to the Markov model of total hip replacement prostheses, or to a partitioned survival model rebuilt from published Kaplan–Meier curves; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
