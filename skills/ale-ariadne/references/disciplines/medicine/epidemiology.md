# Medicine: epidemiology and causal inference

This file adds content specific to epidemiology and causal inference to `references/disciplines/medicine.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: observational study design (cohort and case-control studies, target trial emulation, self-controlled and case-crossover designs), bias, confounding and sensitivity analysis (propensity scores and weighting, instrumental variables and Mendelian randomization, quantitative bias analysis and E-values), surveillance and outbreak investigation (syndromic surveillance, wastewater surveillance, outbreak detection and contact tracing), infectious disease modeling (compartmental and agent-based models, short-term epidemic forecasting, genomic epidemiology and transmission trees), and meta-analysis and network meta-analysis. For the design of randomized trials, external controls and surrogate endpoints see `clinical-trials.md`; for vaccine effectiveness and pathogen typing see `infectious-disease.md`; for burden of disease and environmental exposure see `global-environmental-health.md`; for polygenic risk scores see `precision-medicine.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: the causal question comes down to an effect estimate under a protocol written in advance, and the result of a randomized trial or negative and positive controls exist to compare against. An example is Mendelian randomization from public GWAS summary statistics to decide whether HDL cholesterol is a cause of coronary heart disease: the data can be downloaded directly, the two-sample workflow is mature, and the randomized trials of CETP inhibitors exist to compare against.
- Build with priority: short-term epidemic forecasting, for example quantile forecasts of weekly influenza hospital admissions by state for the coming weeks. The forecast hubs have fixed the output format (23 quantiles) and the scoring rule (WIS and prediction interval coverage) and maintain a baseline model, and the target data is public; the agent judge can rerun the submitted code on forecast dates the solving agent has not seen.
- Build with priority: reconstruction of transmission parameters from public outbreak line lists, for example estimating the serial interval and the reproduction number from the case and contact data of the 2015 MERS outbreak in South Korea; or recomputation of a network meta-analysis given the included studies and the extracted data. The former can first be checked on simulated outbreaks whose true values are known, and the latter has a reference review to compare against.
- Defer: tasks that emulate a target trial in insurance claims or electronic health record data and then compare with the corresponding randomized trial, for example RCT-DUPLICATE, which emulated 32 trials in three US claims databases. Such data is licensed for a fee or under controlled access and cannot be put into a task package. Discuss with the expert switching to a cohort the expert may compliantly put into a task, or to a simulated cohort whose true effect is known, so that only the design and the analysis are examined (alignment of time zero, new-user design, handling of time-varying confounding).
- Defer: causal conclusions that rest on a single observational cohort with neither a trial nor a control outcome to compare against, for example whether the association of a dietary factor with a chronic disease is causal; questions that can be judged only by a new field investigation or prospective follow-up (physical-world observation level 3); modeling aimed at raising the transmissibility of a pathogen, which touches biosafety-sensitive content and is not built.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The full statement of the causal question: target population, treatment strategies and comparator, outcome, start and length of follow-up; whether the estimand is the intention-to-treat effect or the per-protocol effect, and whether it is expressed as a risk difference, a risk ratio or a hazard ratio.
- The identification strategy and its assumptions: what the adjustment set rests on (a causal diagram, consensus in the literature, or an assumption), the three instrumental-variable assumptions, the assumption a self-controlled design makes about confounders that do not vary over time; whether the field accepts this strategy.
- Where the conclusion used for comparison comes from (the corresponding randomized trial, a negative control, a positive control), and whether its population and estimand correspond to those of the observational analysis.
- Mendelian randomization: the version, ancestry and sample overlap of the exposure and outcome GWAS; the significance threshold for instruments and the parameters of linkage disequilibrium clumping; the unit of the effect (per 1 SD or per 1 mmol/L).
- Infectious disease models: the model structure and the parameters fixed in advance (generation interval distribution, reporting delay, reporting fraction), the cutoff date and revision of the data, and whether the forecast target is defined by date of report or date of onset.
- Evidence synthesis: inclusion and exclusion criteria, the effect measure and model (fixed effect or random effects, the estimator of the heterogeneity variance), and the tool used to assess risk of bias.
- Which sensitivity analyses are mandatory (E-value, quantitative bias analysis, negative controls, leave-one-out analysis), and which reporting guideline the report is held to (STROBE-MR, TARGET, PRISMA).
- The form of the conclusion (an effect estimate with its interval, a judgement on whether a causal effect exists, a predictive distribution, a posterior distribution of parameters), and what the tolerance rests on: the confidence interval of the reference analysis, the spread between robust methods, the score of the baseline model.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/medicine.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Effect estimation on simulated truth | The agent judge generates several cohorts (including cases with no effect) from a data-generating mechanism not disclosed in the task statement and runs the submitted analysis code: bias, confidence interval coverage and the rejection rate under no effect fall within tolerance; the tolerance is set from the Monte Carlo standard error of the repeated simulation runs | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Benchmark against the randomized trial | The observational estimate is compared with the estimate of the corresponding trial: direction and statistical significance agree, the point estimate falls within the trial's 95% confidence interval, or the standardized difference between the two does not exceed a bound fixed in advance | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Negative and positive controls | The same analysis pipeline is applied to exposure–outcome pairs known to have no effect and to pairs known to have an effect: the interval of the former contains the null, and the latter has the correct direction; on failure, the conclusion of the main analysis scores nothing | Must pass |
| Recomputation of the Mendelian randomization | The agent judge harmonizes the alleles again from the submitted instrument table and re-estimates: the IVW estimate agrees with the value the submitter reports; the F statistic of each instrument, the heterogeneity test, the MR-Egger intercept and the direction of at least two robust estimators agree with the reference analysis | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Scoring of probabilistic forecasts | The agent judge reruns the submitted code on held-out forecast dates, with the data version available on each date as input: it computes the WIS relative to the baseline model and the coverage of the 50% and 95% prediction intervals | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Recovery of transmission parameters | On simulated outbreak data, the intervals for the reproduction number, the generation interval and other parameters cover the true values; on real outbreak data, they overlap the intervals of the reference analysis | Pure computation, or uses existing data |
| Consistency of a network meta-analysis | From the table of extracted data, the agent judge recomputes the pooled effects, the heterogeneity variance and the ranking of interventions, tests the inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence, and compares with the reference analysis | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |

## Sources of existing data

- Benchmarks with a known true effect: the semi-synthetic data of the ACIC data challenges (covariates taken from real data, treatment and outcome simulated); NHEFS (the cohort on smoking cessation and weight change used in the textbook by Hernán and Robins, public) can serve to demonstrate a workflow. A task whose true value comes from simulation counts as pure computation, not existing data.
- GWAS summary statistics: the results of consortia such as GLGC (blood lipids) and CARDIoGRAMplusC4D (coronary artery disease) can be downloaded directly and must be acknowledged as the consortia require; IEU OpenGWAS has required registration and a token since May 2024, with a query allowance; the GWAS Catalog is a further source.
- Epidemic surveillance and forecasting: the forecasts and target data of past seasons published on GitHub by FluSight and the COVID-19 Forecast Hub; CDC's NHSN hospital admission data and NWSS wastewater surveillance data; the outbreak line lists collected in the R package `outbreaks`.
- Pathogen genomes: sequences in GenBank are public; GISAID requires registration and its terms do not allow sequences to be redistributed, so they cannot be put into a task package.
- Claims and electronic health record cohorts (Optum, MarketScan, Medicare, CPRD) are licensed for a fee or need an application and cannot be put into a task package; the OHDSI tools can run on OMOP data held locally by the expert, but the data cannot be submitted with the task.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The conclusions and values of classic Mendelian randomization analyses, such as HDL, LDL and coronary heart disease, were published long ago and can be answered by searching. Make the submitted instrument table and code the object of scoring, recomputed by the agent judge; and switch to a data version, an outcome or a multivariable specification that the papers did not use.
- The true values of a retrospective forecast are already public. The solving environment has no network access and provides only the data version available on the forecast date; during review the submitted code is rerun on forecast dates not listed in the task statement, and the submitted forecast values are not scored directly.
- The forecast files submitted to a hub by other teams can be downloaded, and copying the ensemble model would earn a high score. Such files must not be placed in the solving environment.
- The result of the trial that a target trial emulation is compared with is already published. The task statement contains neither the trial's name nor its registration number, and the scoring includes quantities that cannot be found by searching, such as covariate balance and negative controls.
- The data-generating code and the true values of a simulated cohort are kept only in `tests/`; during review the cohorts are generated again with different random seeds and parameters, so that the analysis cannot be tuned to data already seen.

## Tools and compute

- Causal inference: in R, `WeightIt`, `MatchIt`, `TrialEmulation`, `gfoRmula` (the g-formula), `SCCS` (self-controlled case series), `EValue`, `episensr`; the HADES tool set of OHDSI.
- Mendelian randomization: `TwoSampleMR`, `MendelianRandomization`, MR-PRESSO; linkage disequilibrium clumping uses PLINK with the 1000 Genomes reference panel.
- Infectious disease modeling and forecasting: `EpiEstim`, `EpiNow2`, Stan; forecasts are scored with `scoringutils` and the hubverse package `hubEvals`; transmission trees and phylogenetics use `outbreaker2`, BEAST 2 and Nextstrain.
- Evidence synthesis: `netmeta`, `multinma` (network meta-analysis).
- Compute: Mendelian randomization from summary statistics and meta-analysis finish within minutes on a single CPU core; a simulation check needs hundreds to thousands of repetitions, on the order of several core-hours; Bayesian infectious disease models and phylogenetic inference take hours to days. Rerunning the submitted code during review costs as much as solving or less. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- Time zero not aligned: eligibility, start of exposure and start of follow-up do not coincide, or people already on treatment are included, which produces immortal time bias and selection bias. When a target trial was emulated explicitly in the electronic health records of 733,804 UK adults, the difference in 10-year cancer-free survival for statins was only −0.5%, whereas earlier analytic approaches showed a strong protective effect.
- Adjustment for variables measured after exposure (mediators or colliders); a propensity score model judged by its discrimination instead of by covariate balance.
- In Mendelian randomization, alleles not harmonized, palindromic variants not handled, or the units of the exposure and outcome effects inconsistent; sample overlap and weak instruments bias the estimate toward the observational association.
- Horizontal pleiotropy left unhandled. An unrestricted HDL allele score suggested protection against coronary heart disease (odds ratio 0.53); after removal of variants also associated with other lipid traits it was 0.91, with an interval spanning 1.
- Only the IVW estimate reported, without heterogeneity and robust estimators. The authors of MR-PRESSO detected horizontal pleiotropy in over 48% of significant causal relationships.
- Forecasts scored with later-revised data as input, or point forecasts evaluated without the intervals; absolute WIS summed across locations, so that the result is dominated by the most populous ones.
- The reproduction number estimated without handling reporting delay and right truncation, so that recent values are systematically too low; a generation interval distribution taken from another pathogen or another phase of the epidemic.
- Disagreement between an observational estimate and a trial judged directly as an error. Their populations, comparators and estimands may differ: in RCT-DUPLICATE the correlation was 0.93 for the 16 trials emulated more closely and 0.53 for the other 16.
- A tolerance set tighter than the uncertainty of the reference analysis itself. The tolerance on an effect estimate should not be smaller than the standard error of the reference analysis, and the tolerance on a simulation metric not smaller than two to three times the Monte Carlo standard error.

## Example: Mendelian randomization of HDL cholesterol and coronary heart disease

In observational studies, each 1 SD higher HDL cholesterol goes with an odds ratio of 0.62 for myocardial infarction. In 2012 Voight and colleagues used genetic variants as instrumental variables to test whether the association is causal: the LIPG Asn396Ser variant, which raises only HDL (20 studies, 20,913 cases and 95,407 controls), raises HDL by 0.14 mmol/L and by the observational association should lower risk by 13%, yet the observed odds ratio was 0.99 (95% confidence interval 0.88–1.11); a genetic score of 14 variants gave an odds ratio of 0.93 (0.68–1.26), whereas the genetic score for LDL gave 2.13 (1.69–2.69), in the same direction as the observational estimate. Later the CETP inhibitor dalcetrapib, which raises HDL, did not reduce cardiovascular events in a randomized trial of 15,871 patients (hazard ratio 1.04, 0.93–1.16). The task asks the solving agent to reach this conclusion independently from public GWAS summary statistics.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives GWAS summary statistics files for blood lipids (HDL cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, triglycerides) and coronary heart disease, a linkage disequilibrium reference panel and fixed selection parameters (P < 5×10⁻⁸, r² < 0.001, window 10,000 kb), and gives no instrument list, no literature values and no names of consortia or papers. The deliverables are an instrument table for each exposure (variant, effect allele, effects on exposure and outcome with standard errors), a `results.json` collecting the estimates, the analysis code and a short report. The format of the instrument table and of `results.json` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Positive control: the causal effect of LDL cholesterol on coronary heart disease, confirmed by the randomized trials of statins, used to show that the pipeline can detect a real effect.
  2. Univariable estimate for HDL cholesterol: selection and strength of the instruments, the IVW estimate and its heterogeneity.
  3. Handling of pleiotropy: most instruments for HDL also affect LDL and triglycerides; give the adjusted effect from robust estimators and multivariable Mendelian randomization, and judge whether a causal effect exists.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the effect of each variant on coronary heart disease predicted from the estimated causal effect, with residual heterogeneity comparable to sampling error) and as text (the variants used and their effects in the two sets of summary statistics; the argument for the three instrumental-variable assumptions, the F statistics, and a range for the estimate instead of a single value where pleiotropy cannot be excluded). The causal claim of sub-question 3, "the unadjusted HDL effect comes from pleiotropy", is supported by ablation: remove the variants associated with other lipid traits, or add and remove LDL and triglycerides in the model, and observe how the estimate changes.
- **Checks.** The agent judge harmonizes and re-estimates from the submitted instrument table and checks the values the submitter reports; it runs the submitted code on simulated summary statistics with a known causal effect and planted pleiotropy, and checks the bias and interval coverage of the estimate; it then compares with the reference analysis: the effect of LDL is positive with an interval excluding zero, the interval for HDL after multivariable adjustment contains zero, and the log odds ratios differ from the reference analysis by no more than twice its standard error. The values of Voight and colleagues and the trial results serve only as a plausibility check; the data versions differ, and numerical agreement is not required. The tolerances are provisional and are set from the spread between robust methods observed in step 2.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); literature conclusions and values copied without an instrument table, or an instrument table whose recomputation disagrees with the reported values (a shortcut; the corresponding values score 0); a submission that completes only the univariable IVW, does not handle pleiotropy and concludes that HDL is protective (a wrong solution consistent with the data, partially correct); the reference analysis itself (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change the exposure and outcome (for example C-reactive protein and coronary heart disease, or body mass index and type 2 diabetes); or switch to target trial emulation: estimate the per-protocol effect in a simulated cohort whose true effect is known, with bias and coverage as the checks.
