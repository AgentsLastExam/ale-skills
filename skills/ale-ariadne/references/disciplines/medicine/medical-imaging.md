# Medicine: medical imaging

This file adds content specific to medical imaging to `references/disciplines/medicine.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: acquisition and reconstruction (accelerated MRI and compressed sensing, low-dose CT and iterative reconstruction, deep-learning reconstruction), segmentation and quantitative imaging (organ and lesion segmentation, quantitative parameter maps and normative modeling), radiomics and prognostic modeling (feature extraction and its reproducibility, imaging-based outcome prediction), registration and multimodal fusion (deformable registration and atlases, fusing imaging with clinical and omic data), and image-guided intervention (treatment planning and dose distributions). For digital pathology slides see `pathology.md`; for physiological signals such as ECG and EEG see `physiological-signals.md`; for prediction models built mainly on electronic health records and their deployment see `health-informatics.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: reconstruction questions for which fully sampled raw data exist, so that the data can be undersampled retrospectively and the result compared with the fully sampled reference image. An example is the reconstruction of multi-coil knee or brain MRI at 4-fold acceleration: the forward model (coil sensitivities and an undersampled Fourier operator) is explicit, and the practice of the fastMRI challenges, ranking by SSIM first and then review by radiologists, can serve as a reference.
- Build with priority: segmentation and detection questions compared against held-out expert annotations, where the difference between several annotators gives a ceiling. An example is the detection and segmentation of lung nodules on chest CT: every scan in LIDC-IDRI was annotated by four thoracic radiologists in two phases, and the difference between annotators is the ceiling the metric can reach.
- Build with priority: questions about the reproducibility of quantitative features. An example is whether radiomic features are stable between repeat scans: RIDER Lung CT holds same-day repeat CT scans of 32 patients with non-small cell lung cancer, IBSI has reached agreement on the definitions and reference values of 169 features, and the criterion comes down to an intraclass correlation coefficient.
- Defer: questions that predict survival, treatment response or gene status from radiomic or deep-learning features with only a single-center cohort. Feature values change with the scanner, the reconstruction kernel and the slice thickness, so the conclusion is hard to re-test externally. Discuss with the expert a formulation that fixes the feature definitions and preprocessing in advance and re-tests a given signature on a public cohort from another center.
- Defer: questions that can be judged only by prospectively undersampled acquisition, a reader study or actual treatment, for example whether an 8-fold accelerated reconstruction can be used for clinical diagnosis (in the 2020 fastMRI challenge several radiologists stated that none of the submissions in the 8-fold track would be acceptable), or the effect of automatic contouring on radiotherapy outcomes. These can be recast as quantitative metrics under retrospective undersampling plus a check that known lesions are preserved; dose prediction can be recast as a comparison with the dose distributions of clinical plans on public planning data such as OpenKBP.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The imaging modality and acquisition conditions: for MRI the sequence, contrast, field strength, number of coils and undersampling scheme (equispaced or random, acceleration factor, size of the fully sampled center); for CT the dose level, reconstruction kernel and slice thickness.
- Where the reference standard comes from: a fully sampled reconstruction (root sum of squares over coils, or a sensitivity-weighted combination), expert contours (how many annotators and how they are merged) or phantom truth; the noise of the reference standard itself and the agreement between annotators.
- The metrics and the details of their computation: the window and dynamic range of SSIM, whether the background is masked, whether it is computed per slice or per volume; how Dice and surface distances treat small targets and empty annotations; how several metrics are aggregated.
- The unit of splitting (patient, examination or slice), and how the held-out data differ from the development data in vendor, field strength and center.
- The feature definitions and preprocessing in radiomics: voxel resampling, gray-level discretization (fixed bin width or fixed bin number), whether they conform to IBSI, and who drew the region of interest.
- The data agreement: whether raw k-space data and challenge data may be put into a task package, and whether only individual registered download is allowed.
- For radiotherapy dose tasks: the prescription dose, the constraints on organs at risk, the dose calculation algorithm and grid, and the criteria of the gamma analysis (for example 3%/2 mm with a 10% dose threshold).
- The form of the conclusion: metric values, a ranking of methods, whether a lesion is preserved, or a reproducibility grade of features; what the tolerance rests on (variation between repeat scans, the difference between annotators, the sensitivity of the metric to implementation details).

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/medicine.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Re-evaluation of a retrospectively undersampled reconstruction | The agent judge undersamples held-out fully sampled data with the mask fixed in the task statement, runs the submitted reconstruction program, computes SSIM, NMSE and PSNR with a fixed implementation, and compares them with zero-filled and compressed-sensing baselines; the values the submitter reports are not used | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Data consistency | The reconstructed image is passed through the forward model back to k-space, and its residual against the measured data at the sampled locations is at noise level; the noise level is estimated from a noise prescan or from the edge of k-space | Must pass |
| Preservation and fabrication of known features | Synthetic lesions of known position and contrast are inserted into held-out raw data, or a digital phantom is used: the contrast recovery of the lesions after reconstruction is computed, and the image is checked for structures absent from the truth | Uses existing data, counts for part of the score |
| Segmentation and detection relative to annotator agreement | Dice, normalized surface distance and false positives per case against held-out expert annotations, compared with the same metrics between pairs of annotators; metrics are aggregated per case with confidence intervals | Uses existing data |
| Reproducibility of features between repeat scans | With fixed feature definitions, the agent judge extracts the features again from the images of two scans of the same patient, computes the intraclass correlation coefficient with its confidence interval, and compares with the submitted grades | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Anatomical consistency of registration | Target registration error on held-out anatomical landmarks, Dice of the propagated annotations, and the fraction of voxels where the Jacobian determinant of the deformation field is non-positive | Uses existing data |
| Comparison of dose distributions | The submitted dose distribution is compared with the reference plan or with a recomputed dose: the gamma passing rate, and the deviations of the dose-volume histogram metrics | Pure computation, or uses existing data |

## Sources of existing data

- Raw data: fastMRI (fully sampled multi-coil k-space data of knee, brain and other anatomy from NYU Langone Health; each user must apply and accept a data sharing agreement, use is limited to internal research and education, and the data may not be redistributed without permission, so they cannot be put into a task package); M4Raw (multi-channel brain k-space data at 0.3 T from 183 healthy volunteers, under a CC BY 4.0 license); LDCT-and-Projection-data (CT projection data with full-dose and low-dose images from Mayo Clinic, hosted on TCIA; the chest and abdomen data are under a CC BY 4.0 license, while the head data could be used to reconstruct a face and are under controlled access).
- Segmentation and detection: LIDC-IDRI (1,018 chest CT cases from 1,010 patients, under a CC BY 3.0 license); the data of challenges such as BraTS and KiTS. For most challenges on grand-challenge.org the test annotations are not public and a score can be obtained only by submitting, so they cannot serve as the held-out annotations of a review.
- Reproducibility and radiomics: RIDER Lung CT (under a CC BY 4.0 license); the digital phantom and feature reference values of IBSI; NSCLC-Radiomics (pretreatment CT, contours and survival data of 422 patients, under a CC BY-NC 3.0 license).
- Registration and radiotherapy: the data of the tasks of the Learn2Reg challenge; OpenKBP (intensity-modulated radiotherapy planning data of 340 patients with head-and-neck cancer, split into 200 for training, 40 for validation and 100 for testing).
- For data simulated from a digital phantom (the Shepp–Logan phantom, BrainWeb), the truth is computed, so the task counts as pure computation, not existing data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- Public fully sampled data are the answer. An input obtained by retrospectively undersampling public data can be matched file by file against the originals to recover the truth. Take held-out volumes from unpublished scans, or insert synthetic lesions in k-space, and change the mask and the acceleration factor; the task statement contains no file names or scan identifiers.
- The annotations of public datasets and the scores on challenge leaderboards can be downloaded; the annotations of LIDC-IDRI are released together with the data. Keep the annotations used for scoring in the agent judge's material only, or have the expert annotate unpublished cases separately.
- One patient has many slices, several sequences and several examinations, and the two scans in a repeat-scan dataset also belong to one patient. Split by patient, never by slice or by sequence.
- Pretrained weights may already have seen the held-out data: public segmentation models, winning challenge weights and foundation models are mostly trained on public data. Restrict the weights that may be used, or confirm that the held-out data are not in their training sets.
- DICOM headers, file names and directory structure can leak the center, the device, the date and even the class; remove them uniformly before providing the data. Metrics are computed by the agent judge rerunning the submitted program; the values the submitter reports are not used.

## Tools and compute

- Reconstruction: BART (BSD license, with ESPIRiT sensitivity estimation and compressed-sensing reconstruction), SigPy, the fastMRI code repository (with U-Net and end-to-end variational network baselines); the ASTRA Toolbox for CT reconstruction.
- Segmentation and registration: nnU-Net (Apache-2.0 license) is the accepted baseline for segmentation tasks; TotalSegmentator; ANTs, elastix and VoxelMorph; 3D Slicer and ITK-SNAP for viewing and contouring.
- Radiomics and metrics: PyRadiomics (feature extraction, which can be checked against the IBSI reference values); the SSIM implementation of scikit-image (the fastMRI challenges used its default parameters); Metrics Reloaded gives recommendations for choosing metrics by task.
- Radiotherapy: matRad (an open-source treatment planning system), pymedphys (gamma analysis); clinical treatment planning systems need a commercial license.
- Compute: training a multi-coil reconstruction network is on the order of days to weeks of GPU time (the finalist models of the 2020 fastMRI challenge were trained for 7–21 days on 1–8 GPUs), and a compressed-sensing reconstruction takes minutes per volume; review runs only inference and metric computation, minutes to hours for tens of volumes; raw k-space data are very large, and the fastMRI multi-coil knee training set alone is close to 1 TB. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- Generating the data and reconstructing them with the same discrete model, or simulating k-space from public images that were already cropped, coil-combined or compressed: the hidden preprocessing inflates the metrics of an undersampled reconstruction. Use real multi-coil raw data.
- High SSIM and PSNR do not guarantee that pathology is shown faithfully. In the 2020 fastMRI challenge, reconstructions that fabricated a vessel or a sulcus still had slice-level SSIM of 0.94–0.96. Scoring must include a check that known features are preserved.
- Metrics are sensitive to implementation details. The background noise of the reference image enters SSIM, and a submission that set the background to zero lost about 0.3 for that reason; the dynamic range, the window size and whether SSIM is computed per slice or per volume must all be fixed in the task statement.
- A conclusion from retrospective undersampling stated as a conclusion about prospectively accelerated acquisition. Retrospective undersampling leaves out differences such as relaxation along the echo train.
- Evaluation only on data from the same vendor as the training data. In the cross-vendor track of the 2020 challenge, the single difference that the raw data lacked frequency oversampling was enough to lower SSIM by 0.1–0.4.
- Dice applied to small lesions or empty annotations. On a small target a difference of one voxel causes a large swing, Dice is undefined when the annotation is empty, and voxel-wise pooling hides small lesions that were missed; choose metrics by task, and report a boundary metric and per-lesion detection as well.
- The contour of a single annotator taken as truth, with a tolerance set tighter than the difference between annotators.
- Radiomic features strongly correlated with lesion volume and left uncorrected; features of the same name having different values in software not checked against IBSI; conclusions on reproducibility that change with slice thickness, reconstruction kernel and gray-level discretization settings.
- Registration reported by Dice alone, without checking the deformation field. A deformation field with folding (non-positive Jacobian determinant) can still reach a very high overlap; the gamma passing rate likewise changes with the criteria and the normalization, and passing rates under different criteria cannot be compared.

## Example: multi-coil MRI reconstruction at 4-fold and 8-fold acceleration

MRI acquires the phase-encoding lines of k-space one at a time; acquiring fewer lines shortens the scan, but the image is then no longer determined uniquely by the data, and reconstruction becomes an underdetermined inverse problem. The fastMRI project released large-scale fully sampled multi-coil raw data and held two challenges: the knee in 2019 (1,594 examinations, 33 submissions) and the brain in 2020 (6,970 scans, of which 3,001 at 1.5 T and 3,969 at 3 T; 19 submissions from 8 groups). Evaluation had two steps: ranking by SSIM against the fully sampled reference image, then review of the top three by radiologists (six in 2020) on how well pathology was depicted. In 2020 the mean SSIM of the top three was 0.964, 0.960 and 0.959 in the 4-fold track and 0.952, 0.944 and 0.942 in the 8-fold track; in 16 of the 18 reviewed cases the reconstruction with the highest SSIM also received the best rating from the radiologists. The organizers also showed reconstructions with high SSIM that fabricated a vessel or a sulcus, and noted that in 2019 all submissions began to miss pathology at 8-fold acceleration. The task asks the solving agent to reconstruct images at given acceleration factors and to state which content of the image is determined by the data.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives undersampled multi-coil k-space data for a number of volumes, the sampling masks, the size of the fully sampled center and the acceleration factors (4-fold and 8-fold), together with fully sampled data for development; the fully sampled data of the held-out volumes and the positions of the inserted lesions are kept in the agent judge's material only. The deliverables are a runnable reconstruction program, the reconstructed image and a pixel-wise uncertainty map for every held-out volume, a `results.json` and a short report. The file format of the reconstructed images (same matrix size and orientation as the reference image) is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Coil sensitivities and the forward model: estimate the coil sensitivities from the fully sampled center, and write the forward operator from the image to undersampled multi-coil k-space.
  2. Reconstruction at 4-fold acceleration: give the reconstructed images and state the prior used (sparsity, low rank or a learned prior).
  3. Identifiability at 8-fold acceleration: state which content of the image is determined by the data and which comes from the prior, and give the uncertainty.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the reconstruction is passed through the forward model back to k-space, and the residual at the sampled locations is at noise level; reconstruction is also correct on a digital phantom and on data with known inserted lesions) and as text (the observed features relied on: the fully sampled center, the sampled phase-encoding lines, the noise prescan; an argument on which spatial frequencies cannot be determined from the data at this acceleration factor because of noise amplification and the null space of the undersampling, with the difference between reconstructions under different initial values and prior strengths as the uncertainty). The constraint that the combined image agrees with the data of every coil is checked by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge runs the submitted program, computes SSIM, NMSE and PSNR against the fully sampled reference image with a fixed implementation, and compares them with zero-filled and compressed-sensing baselines; it confirms that the residual at the sampled locations is at noise level and not near zero; for the inserted synthetic lesions it computes the contrast recovery, and it looks for structures that appear in the reconstruction but not in the reference image; the submitted uncertainty map must cover the regions where the actual error is large. The tolerance is set from the variation of the metrics of one method across volumes in trial runs; SSIM differences smaller than that variation are not ranked (in the 2020 challenge the mean SSIM of the second and third places differed by only 0.001–0.002, and the radiologists ranked them the other way round).
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); a zero-filled reconstruction (a baseline, the low-resolution reconstruction); an image with zero residual at the sampled locations and unsampled locations filled arbitrarily by a generative model (a wrong solution consistent with the data, which fails the inserted-lesion check); public fully sampled data found by search and submitted directly (a shortcut, which fails because the held-out data are unpublished and contain inserted lesions); a submission that completes only 4-fold acceleration (partially correct); a public baseline such as the end-to-end variational network (a known result, with the expected score set by the expert).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Projection-domain reconstruction of low-dose CT (the chest and abdomen data of LDCT-and-Projection-data) and sparse-view CT reconstruction; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
