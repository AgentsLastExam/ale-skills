# Biology: evolution and ecology

This file adds content specific to evolution and ecology to `references/disciplines/biology.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: population genetics and selection (selection scans and adaptive sweeps, quantitative genetics and polygenic adaptation, ancient DNA and demographic history), phylogenomics and comparative genomics (large-scale reconstruction of species trees, genome duplication and rearrangement), species interaction and community ecology (food webs and trophic dynamics, coexistence theory and niche structure, global change and range shifts), behavior and cognitive ecology (social behavior and collective movement, animal learning and cognition), and paleobiology and deep time (fossil morphology and phylogeny, mass extinction and recovery). For the development and evaluation of inference algorithms as such, including tree building, molecular clocks and phylodynamics, see `computational-biology.md`; for the evolution of microbial communities and pathogens see `microbiology-virology.md`; for association analysis and genomic selection in crop populations see `plant-biology.md`; for the evolution of developmental processes see `developmental-biology.md`; for the neural basis of behavior see `systems-neuroscience.md`; for the genetic epidemiology of human disease see `references/disciplines/medicine.md`; for paleoclimate and stratigraphic dating see `references/disciplines/earth.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: questions in population genetics in which simulation can supply the truth for what is inferred. An example is inferring changes in population size, divergence times and migration rates from genomic variation in several populations: coalescent simulation is mature, stdpopsim holds published demographic models that have been checked, and the performance of the inference methods on simulated data has been compared systematically.
- Build with priority: questions in population and community dynamics whose conclusions can be scored on later observations that are held out. An example is forecasting the abundance of desert rodents, or aquatic and terrestrial ecological variables, from long-term monitoring series: the data keep being updated, and forecasts are evaluated with scoring rules such as CRPS and compared with naive baselines such as climatology and persistence.
- Build with priority: distribution models and diversity reconstructions that have independent evaluation data. Examples are building a species distribution model from presence-only records and evaluating it on an independent presence–absence survey, or reconstructing a sampling-standardized diversity curve from fossil occurrence records.
- Defer: deep nodes of the phylogeny that are still disputed in the field and whose resolution changes with the model and the data processing, for example whether ctenophores or sponges are the sister group of all other animals. Discuss with the expert a reformulation that fixes the alignment and the substitution model in advance and asks for the support of a specific node and its sensitivity to the model and to taxon sampling.
- Defer: conclusions about adaptation that rest on statistical signals from which confounding is hard to exclude, for example polygenic adaptation inferred from GWAS effect sizes (the selection signal for height in Europeans weakened greatly in data with better control of population stratification); and questions that can only be judged after a new field season or a new animal behavior experiment (physical-world observation level 3).

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- Species and samples: the number of individuals, what the population labels rest on, where and when the samples were taken; for ancient DNA, the age and coverage of the samples and the treatment of damage.
- The mutation rate, the recombination rate (or genetic map) and the generation time used for conversion, with their uncertainties; whether conclusions are expressed in coalescent units or in years and numbers of individuals.
- The form and filtering of the data: phased or not, how the ancestral allele is determined, the accessibility mask, the handling of low-coverage sites and missing data.
- The class of demographic model: the number of populations, whether migration and admixture are allowed, whether population sizes are piecewise constant or change exponentially; whether factors outside the model (linked selection, structure within populations) are included.
- For a phylogenomic dataset, how orthologs are identified, how alignments are built and trimmed, the choice of outgroups, and the choice between concatenation and coalescent methods.
- The observation process of ecological data: survey effort, detection probability, historical changes of sampling method; whether abundance is a count, a catch or an index.
- For fossil data, the taxonomic level of identification, the time binning and the method of sampling standardization.
- The form of the conclusion (parameters, a tree topology, a forecast distribution, a set of selected loci) and what the tolerance rests on (spread between replicate simulations, bootstrap intervals, observation error).

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Parameter recovery on simulated truth | The agent judge simulates data again under the same class of model with undisclosed parameters; the parameters that the submitted inference pipeline returns on them are compared with the truth by relative error, and the coverage of the declared intervals is checked | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Recomputation of forward statistics | The expected site frequency spectrum or linkage disequilibrium statistics are recomputed from the submitted demographic model, and the residual against the data is at the level of sampling noise set by a block bootstrap; statistics not used in fitting are compared separately | Pure computation, must pass |
| Power and false positives of a selection scan | Thresholds are set by neutral simulations under the fitted demographic history and the false positive rate is reported; selected loci planted in the simulation, or accepted positive loci, must be detected | Pure computation, or uses existing data |
| Comparison of tree topology | The reconstructed tree is compared with the simulated truth or a reference tree by the normalized Robinson–Foulds distance and the quartet distance; the support of specified nodes and the gene tree concordance are recomputed by the agent judge | Pure computation, or uses existing data |
| Forecast scoring on a held-out period | A probabilistic forecast submitted before the cutoff date is scored with CRPS on the observations that follow, and must beat the climatology and persistence baselines | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Spatially independent evaluation of a distribution model | Metrics such as AUC are computed on an independent presence–absence survey or on spatially blocked held-out regions; values obtained with a random split are not accepted | Uses existing data |
| Recomputation of sampling standardization | From the occurrence records the agent judge recomputes diversity, origination rates and extinction rates with a time binning and a standardization method fixed in advance, and the result agrees with the submitted curves | Uses existing data, must pass |

## Sources of existing data

- Population genomics: the 1000 Genomes Project (2,504 individuals from 26 populations), the high-coverage genomes of HGDP and SGDP, the summary frequencies of gnomAD; the DGRP for Drosophila; the AADR compilation of ancient DNA. Cohorts with individual phenotypes are mostly under controlled access.
- Simulation and model catalogs: stdpopsim (genetic maps and published demographic models for more than twenty species). The truth of data generated from it is known, so such data counts as pure computation, not existing data.
- Phylogenetics and comparative genomics: OrthoDB, Ensembl Compara, TreeBASE, the Open Tree of Life, TimeTree.
- Ecological monitoring and distributions: the Portal Project (long-term monitoring of desert rodents, plants and weather in the southwestern United States since 1977, CC0), BioTIME (assemblage time series, open access), NEON, the North American Breeding Bird Survey, GBIF (licenses differ between datasets); eBird requires a request and acceptance of its data access terms, which limit use to non-commercial research and education and forbid redistribution, and studies in Movebank that are not open for download need the consent of the data owner. Benchmark data for species distribution models are in the R package disdat (226 anonymized species in 6 regions, with independent presence–absence evaluation data).
- Fossil record: the occurrence records of the Paleobiology Database.

## Preventing answer leakage

- Published demographic parameters, species trees and significant loci can be found by searching. Take the truth from a new simulation with changed parameters, and do not name in the task statement the published model it is based on.
- The model parameters in the stdpopsim catalog are public, so a task that uses them unchanged can be passed by lookup. Change the population sizes, divergence times and migration rates, or add events the catalog does not contain; the truth and the random seeds are kept only in `tests/`.
- A forecasting task must freeze the data snapshot and the cutoff date and score on observations released after the cutoff. A "forecast" of a historical period can be filled in directly from public data.
- Public benchmarks for distribution models and diversity analysis come with evaluation data. Keep the evaluation data out of the solving environment; where the species in a benchmark are anonymized, keep them anonymized.
- Data from the same individual, the same family or the same plot cannot appear in both the training part and the held-out part; the task statement contains no dataset names, sample identifiers or paper information.

## Tools and compute

- Population genetic simulation: msprime, SLiM, stdpopsim, with output as tskit tree sequences; demographic models can be exchanged in the Demes format.
- Demographic inference: ∂a∂i, moments, momi2 (based on the site frequency spectrum); PSMC, MSMC2, SMC++, Relate (based on sequences or genealogies); fastsimcoal2 is distributed as free executables, and its own page carries neither source code nor a license statement, so check its terms of use.
- Selection and admixture: selscan, SweepFinder2, diploS/HIC; ADMIXTOOLS.
- Phylogenomics: OrthoFinder, MAFFT, trimAl, IQ-TREE, RAxML-NG, ASTRAL, BEAST 2, RevBayes.
- Ecology and paleobiology: the R packages vegan, unmarked, blockCV and divDyn; MaxEnt; PyRate.
- Compute: a coalescent simulation of one human-scale chromosome for tens of samples takes minutes on a single machine, and one fit with a site frequency spectrum method takes minutes to hours and has to be repeated from several initial values; species tree inference and Bayesian dating for hundreds of species and over a thousand genes run for days on a cluster; the cost of forward simulation with selection rises quickly with population size. Review simulates again and recomputes the statistics, usually less than solving. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- The site frequency spectrum determines only parameters scaled by the mutation rate. When the values taken for the mutation rate and the generation time change, population sizes and times are rescaled as a whole; convert to a common basis before comparing with literature values.
- A misspecified model: ignoring migration makes divergence times too recent; the effects of structure within populations or of linked selection are taken as changes in population size.
- Sequence-based methods can return an undulating curve even when the population size is constant; estimates for the most recent and the most ancient periods are limited by sample size and by the resolution of recombination, so the tolerance there should not be tight.
- Composite likelihood treats linked sites as independent, and the confidence intervals obtained from it are too narrow; intervals must come from a block bootstrap or a corresponding variance correction.
- Low coverage, reference bias, phasing errors and misidentified ancestral alleles change rare-variant and haplotype statistics; damage in ancient DNA produces spurious transitions.
- The threshold of a selection scan set with a null model of constant population size. Bottlenecks and expansions can produce signals similar to a selective sweep.
- High bootstrap support does not mean a correct topology. Systematic error from compositional heterogeneity, long-branch attraction and paralogy does not go away as data are added; when concatenation and coalescent methods disagree, report both.
- A catch or a number of records taken as abundance: survey effort and detection probability change over time; the classic lynx and snowshoe hare series is a record of fur returns, not a population census.
- A distribution model evaluated with a random split, so that spatial autocorrelation inflates the metric; a fossil diversity curve without sampling standardization, which reflects the rock record and research intensity.

## Example: out-of-Africa demographic history from genomic variation

Gutenkunst and colleagues fitted a three-population model to the joint site frequency spectrum of an African, a European and an East Asian population: an ancestral population with an effective size of about 7,300 expanded to 12,300 about 220,000 years ago; an out-of-Africa population of about 2,100 split off about 140,000 years ago; about 21,200 years ago this population divided into a European and an East Asian branch, with initial sizes of about 1,000 and 510, which then grew exponentially at 0.4% and 0.55% per generation; migration between the populations is on the order of 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ per generation (with a generation time of 25 years and a mutation rate of 2.35 × 10⁻⁸ per base per generation). The model is now in the stdpopsim catalog and has become a common scenario for testing inference methods. The founders of stdpopsim compared several methods on simulated data: on one of the human models the inferred values were mostly within a factor of two of the truth; sequence-based methods returned sinusoidal fluctuations even when the population size was constant; methods that assume no migration between populations systematically underestimated divergence times; two-population fits based on the site frequency spectrum did not capture the recent exponential growth of the European population. Real data has no ground truth, so whether a method can be trusted can only be judged on simulations in which the truth is known. The task is built on this: the data are simulated with changed parameters, and the solving agent has to infer the demographic history and give its uncertainty.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 0.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives simulated variant data (VCF) for several diploid samples from each of three populations, the recombination map, the mutation rate and the generation time, and states the class of model (three populations, with migration and exponential growth allowed); it does not give the parameter values or the published model they are based on. The deliverables are a demographic model file in the Demes format, `estimates.csv` (point estimates and 95% intervals of the parameters), the inference code and a statement on identifiability. The format of the Demes file and of `estimates.csv` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. The ancestral population and the early expansion: the effective size of the ancestral population, and the time and magnitude of the expansion.
  2. The out-of-Africa bottleneck and the divergences: the size of the bottleneck, the times of the two divergences, and the migration rates after divergence.
  3. Recent growth: the initial sizes and growth rates of the European and East Asian populations.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the statistics recomputed or simulated from the inferred model, with residuals at the level of sampling noise; recovery of the parameters by the same pipeline on another set of simulations with known parameters) and as text (the statistics that constrain this group of parameters, for example the proportion of shared and population-specific variants in the joint site frequency spectrum, the number of rare variants, the decay of linkage disequilibrium with distance; the correlation between divergence time and migration rate and the extent to which sample size limits the recent growth, stated and expressed as intervals). The physical constraints (population sizes and times are positive and in a consistent order) are checked by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge recomputes the expected joint site frequency spectrum from the submitted Demes model with a program fixed in advance and compares it with the data; the gap in log-likelihood to the true model must lie within the range given by a block bootstrap. Each parameter is compared with the truth: for well-identified parameters (the ancestral size, the African size, the time of the out-of-Africa event) the relative error is within a factor fixed in advance, and for poorly identified parameters (migration rates, recent growth rates) only coverage of the truth by the interval is required; statistics not used in fitting (for example the decay of linkage disequilibrium on a held-out chromosome) are compared separately. The tolerances are provisional and are set from the spread of the reference computation over several replicate simulations; they should not be tighter than the factor of two reported in the literature.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); the published parameters copied (a shortcut: the truth has been changed, so both the parameter comparison and the likelihood check fail); a single-population model of constant size (a trivial solution); a divergence model without migration (partially correct: the site frequency spectrum roughly matches, the divergence times are too recent); a model with too many epochs that fits the sampling noise (a higher likelihood, but the held-out statistics disagree); the inference of the reference computation (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change to a demographic model of Drosophila or Arabidopsis, to a model with archaic human admixture, or to locating selective sweeps planted in a simulation under a given demographic history; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
