# Biology: development and tissue biology

This file adds content specific to development and tissue biology to `references/disciplines/biology.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: morphogenesis and patterning (morphogen gradients and their precision, tissue mechanics and vertex models, collective cell migration and jamming), stem cells and fate decisions (lineage tracing and fate maps, reprogramming and pluripotency, niche signaling and asymmetric division), organoids and in vitro development (gastruloids and synthetic embryology, organoid models of organ function), regeneration and repair (wound healing and fibrosis), and the evolution of development (cis-regulatory evolution, comparative embryology across species). For the cytoskeleton, adhesion and migration mechanisms of single cells see `cell-biology.md`; for single-cell analysis methods as such, including trajectory and RNA-velocity inference, see `computational-biology.md`; for the generation and processing of single-cell and spatial omics data see `genomics-omics.md`; for the wiring of neural circuits see `cellular-neuroscience.md`; for plant meristems and organ initiation see `plant-biology.md`; for scaffold materials and tissue engineering see `bioengineering.md`; for organoids used to stratify patients or assess drug response see `references/disciplines/medicine.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: questions in patterning that come down to a measurable precision or scaling relation, with quantitative imaging data to compare against. An example is the readout precision of the Bicoid gradient in the Drosophila embryo: the length constant of the gradient, the absolute concentration in nuclei and the output noise have all been measured, and stochastic models and estimates of the physical limit can be done by pure computation.
- Build with priority: questions in tissue mechanics in which the model output can be compared item by item with image statistics. An example is explaining cell packing in the Drosophila wing disc epithelium with a vertex model: the distribution of polygon classes, the mean area of each class and the recoil after laser ablation have been measured, and after normalization the model has only two parameters, line tension and contractility.
- Build with priority: lineage reconstruction with known ground truth, for example reconstructing cell lineages from CRISPR barcode readouts. In vitro systems give the true lineage by time-lapse imaging, the lineage of C. elegans is invariant, and for simulated data the truth comes from the simulation; the field evaluates with the Robinson–Foulds distance and the triplet distance.
- Defer: questions that can only be judged after days to weeks of culture, for example factor combinations that raise reprogramming efficiency, new differentiation protocols for organoids or gastruloids, interventions in regeneration and fibrosis in vivo (physical-world observation level 3). Discuss with the expert a reformulation as a quantitative conclusion that can be recomputed on public imaging or single-cell data, or as a ranking of protocols within a model fixed in advance.
- Defer: conclusions about lineage and fate that rest only on pseudotime or RNA velocity from single-cell transcriptomes. Such inferences are not lineages, and different methods often disagree; build only when barcodes, imaging or a known fate map provide ground truth.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- Species, strain and developmental stage; what staging rests on (nuclear cycle, somite number, time after fertilization, depth of membrane invagination during cellularization), and whether the time resolution supports the conclusion.
- Position coordinates and their normalization: absolute length or percent of embryo or tissue length; where along the dorsoventral and left–right axes the samples are taken.
- What makes the imaging data quantitative: antibody staining or a live fluorescent fusion protein, whether intensity is linear in concentration, and how measurement noise is separated from variation between individuals.
- The class of model and what is fixed in advance: a reaction–diffusion model, a vertex model or a cellular Potts model; which parameters are given by independent measurements (diffusion constant, protein lifetime, division cycle) and which may be fitted.
- The perturbations used to discriminate between mechanisms (mutants, gene dosage, laser ablation, drug inhibition, embryo size), and which of them are held out for independent prediction.
- Where the lineage ground truth comes from (time-lapse imaging, an invariant lineage, simulation) and how complete it is; the number of barcode sites, the editing rate and the dropout rate.
- The cell lines, batches and differentiation protocols behind organoid and stem-cell data, and on how many lines or batches a conclusion must hold; the ethical and regulatory requirements where human embryo models are involved.
- The form of the conclusion (a precision, a scaling exponent, a choice between mechanisms, a tree topology) and what the tolerance rests on (variation between individuals, measurement noise, spread between seeds of a stochastic simulation).

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Recomputation of the positional error | From the expression profiles (public data or submitted simulation output) the agent judge recomputes the positional error, that is the standard deviation of expression across individuals divided by the local slope of the profile, expressed in percent of embryo length, and compares it with the values the submitter reports and with the measured precision | Pure computation, or uses existing data |
| Rerun of the full model and the ablated models | The submitted model and the versions with one element removed at a time are rerun with fixed random seeds: the full model reproduces the quantitative features of the pattern, and removing a required element makes the feature disappear or change quantitatively | Pure computation, must pass |
| Measured bounds on parameters | The submitted diffusion constants, lifetimes, integration times, division cycles and similar parameters lie within the measured ranges given in the task statement; for a model that reproduces the pattern outside these ranges, the corresponding values score nothing | Must pass |
| Statistics of tissue geometry | From the submitted vertex configurations the agent judge recomputes the distribution of polygon classes, the mean area of each class and the recoil displacement after ablation, and compares them with image measurements; the difference between distributions is measured with a distance fixed in advance | Pure computation, or uses existing data |
| Comparison of a lineage tree with ground truth | The reconstructed lineage tree is compared with the truth from time-lapse imaging, an invariant lineage or simulation, using the normalized Robinson–Foulds distance and the triplet distance, and against a baseline of neighbor joining on barcode distances | Uses existing data, or pure computation, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Prediction for held-out perturbations | For perturbations not used in parameter tuning (gene dosage, mutants, embryo size) the model predicts the shift of pattern features, which is compared with measurements | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |

## Sources of existing data

- Quantitative imaging of patterning: the public Dryad dataset of immunofluorescence profiles of the Drosophila gap genes (163 embryos in nuclear cycle 14; the four genes Hb, Kr, Gt and Kni); the in situ hybridization image collection of BDGP; raw images held by the Image Data Resource and the BioImage Archive.
- Lineage and tracking: the invariant cell lineage of C. elegans (WormBase); the data of the Allen Institute Cell Lineage Reconstruction DREAM Challenge (in vitro intMEMOIR recordings with ground truth from time-lapse imaging, plus simulated lineages of about 1,000 cells for C. elegans and 10,000 cells for mouse), held on Synapse, where download requires registration; the 2D and 3D time-lapse sequences of the Cell Tracking Challenge, whose test-set annotations are not public.
- Developmental atlases: public datasets such as the single-cell atlas of mouse gastrulation and early organogenesis. Data from human embryos and fetal tissue is mostly under controlled access.
- Model organism databases and regulatory sequences: FlyBase, WormBase, ZFIN, MGI, Xenbase; enhancers validated in transgenic assays are in the VISTA Enhancer Browser and REDfly.
- "Reference values" obtained from a vertex model or a reaction–diffusion model count as pure computation, not existing data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The measured values and model parameters of the classic systems are published (the length constant of the gradient, the parameters of the vertex model, the bits of positional information), so a direct comparison of numbers can be passed by searching. Make the submitted runnable model the object of scoring: the agent judge reruns it and recomputes the features.
- Change the parameters given in the task statement (embryo length, nuclear spacing, integration time, gene dosage), or ask for quantities the papers did not report (the precision after an ablation, the shift of a boundary under another perturbation).
- The data of the DREAM lineage challenge and the participants' submissions are public. Generate the truth by simulation with new random seeds and changed editing parameters; when in vitro data is used, provide only the barcode readouts and keep the truth in `tests/` only.
- Cells from the same embryo, the same clone or the same batch of organoids cannot appear in both the training part and the held-out part.
- The task statement contains no dataset names, paper titles, figure numbers or other identifiers that lead back to the answer.

## Tools and compute

- Image segmentation and tracking: Fiji, Cellpose, StarDist, ilastik, napari, TrackMate.
- Mechanical models of tissues and cell populations: Chaste (BSD license, includes vertex models); CompuCell3D and Morpheus (cellular Potts models).
- Reaction–diffusion and stochastic simulation: general solvers for ordinary and partial differential equations (SciPy, FEniCS); Gillespie-type algorithms can be implemented directly.
- Lineage reconstruction: Cassiopeia; neighbor joining on barcode distances serves as a baseline. For pseudotime and RNA-velocity tools see `computational-biology.md`.
- Compute: a one-dimensional gradient with a stochastic readout model, or a two-dimensional vertex model of hundreds to thousands of cells, takes minutes to hours on a single CPU machine; three-dimensional tissues and lineage reconstruction for tens of thousands of cells need a cluster; segmentation and tracking of 3D time-lapse images needs GPUs, and a single dataset can reach tens to hundreds of GB. Review only reruns a few simulations with fixed seeds and is far smaller than solving. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- The steepness of a boundary taken as precision. A high Hill coefficient only sharpens the boundary; it does not reduce the variation of the boundary position between embryos.
- The gradient assumed to be at steady state. Nuclear cycles in the Drosophila syncytial stage last on the order of ten to twenty minutes, so the readout need not happen after steady state is reached.
- Loose staging. The gap gene pattern keeps moving during nuclear cycle 14, so pooling embryos of different ages counts change over time as variation between embryos.
- Immunofluorescence intensity taken directly as concentration, with no check of linearity and no subtraction of imaging noise from the total variance.
- A reaction–diffusion model with many free parameters can fit any single pattern. Different mechanisms give the same wild-type pattern and must be told apart by independent predictions under perturbation.
- Vertex model parameters determined from the distribution of polygon classes alone. Several parameter sets give similar distributions; area statistics or the recoil after laser ablation must be used as well.
- Pseudotime order taken as lineage; or homoplasy, dropout and saturation of barcodes ignored, so that cells with the same readout are judged closely related.
- A lineage tree evaluated by the Robinson–Foulds distance alone. That distance is very sensitive to unresolved branches and to a few misplaced cells; report the triplet distance as well.
- An organoid conclusion drawn from one cell line or one batch; the differences between batches are often larger than the reported effect.

## Example: readout precision of the Bicoid gradient

In the early Drosophila embryo the maternal protein Bicoid forms an approximately exponential concentration gradient from the anterior end, with a length constant λ of about 100 μm in an embryo about 500 μm long; in nuclear cycle 14 neighboring nuclei are about 8 μm apart, and the concentrations they see differ by only about 10%. By live imaging, Gregor and colleagues measured the following: at the boundary of Hunchback expression (about 48% of embryo length) the nuclear concentration of Bicoid is 4.8 ± 0.6 molecules/μm³ (about 8 nM, about 690 molecules per nucleus); the response of Hunchback to Bicoid is described by a Hill coefficient of 5; the noise referred to the input is about 10%, just enough to distinguish neighboring nuclei. By the Berg–Purcell limit, however, a single binding site about 3 nm in size, with a diffusion constant of no more than 1 μm²/s, has to average the concentration over about 7000 s (nearly two hours) to reach 10%, close to the whole time from fertilization to cellularization. The measured noise is correlated between neighboring nuclei, with a correlation length of 5 ± 1 nuclei, which points to spatial averaging; with spatial averaging included, an integration of about 200 s is enough to reach 10%. The task asks the solving agent to build a stochastic model and show quantitatively how this precision is achieved.

The following is illustrated for the Explanation class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 0.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives the measured quantities above (λ, the nuclear spacing, the concentration at the boundary, the Hill coefficient, the upper bound on the diffusion constant), an upper bound on the available integration time set by the expert, and the class of model (stochastic binding of Bicoid to promoter sites, synthesis of Hunchback and its diffusion between nuclei); it does not give the measured correlation length or the conclusions of the literature. The deliverables are runnable model code, output profiles of several simulated embryos for each model version, a `results.json` collecting the values (the noise referred to the input at the boundary δc/c, the standard deviation of the boundary position, the correlation length of the noise), the derivation and a short report. The format of the output profiles and of `results.json` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Time averaging at a single site: the integration time needed to reach 10% at the given concentration, diffusion constant and site size, and how far it is from the available time.
  2. Spatial averaging between nuclei: whether exchange of Hunchback between neighboring nuclei can bring the noise down to 10% within minutes, and the correlation length of the noise that this predicts.
  3. Cooperative binding and noise propagation: how an input–output relation with a Hill coefficient of 5 converts input noise into an error in the boundary position, and whether cooperativity itself reduces noise.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the phenomenon (the precision, concentration and time scales given in the task statement). The deliverable is the mechanism, as computation (a stochastic simulation with no imposed output noise; ablation removes diffusion between nuclei, reduces the binding sites to one, and limits the integration time to the available bound, one at a time, and observes the change in boundary precision) and as mathematics (the Berg–Purcell estimate δc/c ≈ (DacT)^(−1/2), an estimate of the effective number of independent samples under spatial averaging, and the conversion from concentration noise to positional error σx = λ·δc/c). The causal claim "spatial averaging is required to reach 10% within the available time" is supported by ablation.
- **Checks.** The agent judge reruns the submitted full model and each ablated version with fixed random seeds, recomputes δc/c and the standard deviation of the boundary position from the output profiles, and checks the values the submitter reports: for the version with time averaging at a single site only and the integration time at the available bound, δc/c must be clearly above 10%; the full model must reach the order of 10%, that is a positional error of about 10 μm, or 2% of embryo length; the theoretical estimate of the integration time differs from 7000 s by no more than a factor of two; the predicted correlation length of the noise is compared with the held-out measurement of 5 ± 1 nuclei. All parameters must lie within the measured ranges given in the task statement. The tolerances are provisional and are set from the measurement error of the concentration at the boundary (about 12%) and the spread between seeds.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); literature values and conclusions copied without a runnable model (a shortcut; the corresponding values score 0); a model that reaches 10% with a diffusion constant above the measured bound or an integration time of two hours (a shortcut: it reproduces the precision but violates the parameter bounds); a simulation whose output profiles are smoothed afterwards or whose noise amplitude is set by hand (a shortcut, detected by inspecting the trajectory); a submission that completes only sub-question 1 (partially correct); the reference computation with diffusion between nuclei (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change to explaining cell packing in the wing disc epithelium with a vertex model (the parameter set with normalized line tension 0.12 and contractility 0.04, the disorder introduced by cell division, the recoil after laser ablation), or to the positional precision of about 1% of embryo length given jointly by the four gap genes; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
