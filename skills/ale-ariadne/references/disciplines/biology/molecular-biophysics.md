# Biology: molecular biophysics and structure

This file adds content specific to molecular biophysics and structure to `references/disciplines/biology.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: protein folding and energy landscapes, intrinsically disordered proteins, misfolding and aggregation, experimental structure determination (cryo-electron microscopy and tomography, crystallography and serial femtosecond methods, solution and solid-state NMR), molecular dynamics and free energy calculation, protein engineering and de novo design, and the structure of nucleic acids and chromatin. For the generation and analysis of omics data see `genomics-omics.md`; for the development of algorithms and benchmarking pipelines themselves see `computational-biology.md`; for the function and regulation of molecules inside the cell see `cell-biology.md`; for the chemical mechanism of enzyme catalysis and the chemical properties of drug molecules see `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: the question comes down to quantities that can be recomputed from a structure (the fit of coordinates to a density map, the deviation of a structure from a reference structure, relative binding free energies, folding stability differences), and held-out experimental data, a reference computation or the scores of a public assessment exist to compare against. An example is building and validating an atomic model from a public cryo-EM density map: the 2019 EMDataResource model challenge established the practice of using fit metrics and geometry metrics together, and every metric can be recomputed by the agent judge from the submitted coordinates. The relative binding free energy of a congeneric ligand series belongs here as well; a public assessment over 8 targets, 199 ligands and 330 perturbation edges has mean unsigned errors reported between 0.9 and 1.1 kcal/mol depending on the method and on how they are computed.
- Build with priority: structure tasks that work by splitting on time, for example predicting an experimental structure or complex interface released only after the cutoff date, scored by lDDT, TM-score, GDT_TS and interface metrics. The sequence databases, structure databases and model weights in the solving environment must be truncated to the same date, or homologous structures leak the answer directly.
- Defer: questions whose conclusion depends on a conformational ensemble that is hard to pin down, or on a treatment still disputed in the field, for example reconstructing the ensemble of an intrinsically disordered protein, or absolute binding free energies when the net charge changes. For such questions, discuss with the expert fixing the force field, the protonation state and the reference ensemble in advance, and restating the conclusion as a relative ordering within the model.
- Defer: questions that can only be decided by a newly determined experimental structure or a new affinity measurement, when that measurement takes more than six hours (physical-world observation level 3).
- Defer: design tasks that touch biosafety-sensitive content.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- The target quantity and its reference state: a relative binding free energy, a folding stability difference, a conformational free energy surface or structural coordinates; the conventions for standard-state concentration, symmetry numbers and terminal capping.
- Preparation of the system: protonation states and tautomers, metal ions and structural waters, disulfide bonds, box size, ionic strength and how the system is neutralized.
- The force field, water model and the source of the ligand parameters, and whether the field accepts this combination; the sampling protocol: length of a single trajectory, number of independent repeats, number of λ windows, enhanced sampling method and convergence criterion.
- The source of the experimental comparison: data measured in one laboratory by one assay, or values pooled from several sources; whether the measurement conditions (temperature, buffer, pH) correspond to the calculation settings.
- For density-map tasks: whether the full map or a half-map is provided and whether it has been sharpened, the mask and the criterion behind the nominal resolution, the range of local resolution, and the source and quality of the reference model.
- For structure tasks, the cutoff date, and the versions of the sequence databases, structure databases and prediction model weights in the solving environment.
- The form of the conclusion, and what the tolerance rests on: experimental reproducibility, the spread between independent repeats, or the difference between force fields.
- For design tasks: whether the scoring program and thresholds are fixed in advance, and whether realizability is listed as a condition.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Reanalysis of the free energies | From the submitted reduced-potential samples or dH/dλ time series, the agent judge recomputes the free energy of every perturbation edge with MBAR: the result agrees with the values the submitter reports, neighboring λ windows overlap sufficiently, and the uncertainty comes from independent repeats rather than from a single trajectory alone | Pure computation, must pass |
| Thermodynamic cycle closure | The free energies around every closed cycle of the perturbation network sum to zero within statistical error, and forward and reverse directions agree | Must pass |
| Comparison with held-out experimental values | Predicted relative free energies are compared with the held-out experimental values by RMSE and Kendall τ, with confidence intervals from bootstrapping; the tolerance is set jointly by experimental reproducibility and method error | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2 |
| Comparison of an ensemble with observables | Experimental observables are recomputed from the submitted ensemble (NMR scalar couplings and order parameters, SAXS curves), with residuals against the measurements at noise level; a submission fitted with a single conformation that cannot satisfy all observables at once scores nothing | Uses existing data |
| Agreement between model and density map | For cryo-EM tasks: from the submitted coordinates, the agent judge recomputes the map-model FSC at FSC = 0.5, the Q-score and EMRinger, and cross-validates against the held-out half-map; geometry and peptide orientation are checked with MolProbity and CaBLAM. Fit metrics and geometry metrics must both be met | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 1–2 |
| Sufficiency of sampling | The spread between independent repeats does not exceed the stated uncertainty, and block analysis in time shows the target quantity no longer drifting systematically with simulation length | Must pass |
| Design under a fixed scoring program | For design tasks: candidate sequences and structures are scored by a program fixed in advance (self-consistency of the predicted structure, interface metrics, a similarity search against known structures), which the agent judge reruns independently | Scientific discovery level 3 |

## Sources of existing data

- Structures and raw data: the PDB (experimental structures, resolution and refinement statistics), EMDB (cryo-EM density maps and half-maps), EMPIAR (raw electron microscopy data, up to terabytes for one dataset), BMRB (NMR chemical shifts and relaxation data); PDB-REDO provides re-refined models. For a split by time, use the date of first release in the PDB.
- Assessment targets and official scores: the targets and assessment results of past CASP rounds, the weekly evaluations of CAMEO (scored with lDDT, the CAD score, GDT_TS, QS-score and similar metrics), and the target density maps and assessment results of the EMDataResource model challenges.
- Affinities and free energies: protein-ligand-benchmark (curated congeneric ligand series with structures and experimental affinities) and the datasets of the SAMPL blind challenges; BindingDB and ChEMBL pool several sources with inconsistent measurement conditions, so check the origin and assay of each value before using it for comparison.
- When public long trajectories, force field parameter sets or the output of structure prediction models are used for comparison, the reference values are themselves computed, so the task counts as pure computation, not as using existing data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- Published free energies and structures can be found by searching: make the submitted samples, input files and coordinates the object of scoring, let the agent judge recompute them, and let the values the submitter reports score nothing on their own.
- Split structure tasks by time: use only entries released after the cutoff date, and truncate the sequence databases, structure databases and model weights in the solving environment to the same date.
- The task statement contains no PDB IDs, EMDB IDs, UniProt accessions, compound names or paper titles; ligands are renumbered in the task statement.
- The held-out experimental values, the held-out half-map, the reference model and the results of the reference computation are kept only in `tests/` and never enter the solving environment with the task package.
- For design tasks, the agent judge uses the search tool to compare candidates with known entries; a submission of a known molecule or a published design scores nothing.

## Tools and compute

- Molecular dynamics: GROMACS and OpenMM are open source; NAMD comes with source code free of charge but only for non-commercial use, and commercial use needs a separate license; Amber's pmemd needs a license, while AmberTools is open source; Desmond is free to academic and not-for-profit institutions.
- Free energy and enhanced sampling: pmx, OpenFE, alchemlyb and pymbar (MBAR estimation), PLUMED; cinnabar (formerly Arsenic) for consistent reporting of relative free energy results.
- Force fields and parameterization: Amber ff19SB ships with the open-source AmberTools, the OpenFF parameter files are released under CC BY 4.0, and the CHARMM36m parameter files can be downloaded freely but state no license; OPLS4 ships with commercial software; ligand parameterization with openff-toolkit and antechamber.
- Structure assessment and design: OpenStructure (lDDT and related metrics), DockQ, MolProbity, ChimeraX; structure prediction and design with ColabFold, ESMFold, ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion, and Rosetta, which is free for academic use and needs a license for commercial use.
- Building and validating cryo-EM models: Coot, ISOLDE, ModelAngelo, Servalcat, MapQ (Q-score); Phenix (real-space refinement, map-model FSC, EMRinger) is free for academic use and needs a license for commercial use.
- Compute: automated building and refinement of a protein of a few hundred residues into a density map at about 3 Å takes on the order of hours on a workstation with one GPU; recomputing the fit and geometry metrics during review takes only minutes. Free energy perturbation networks and long simulations take several GPUs over several days, one to two orders of magnitude more. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- A wrong protonation state, tautomer or chirality for the ligand, which shifts all the free energies together.
- Only one binding pose sampled where the pose is not unique, so the result is set by the starting structure rather than by the force field.
- Insufficient overlap between λ windows, so the MBAR error is underestimated; uncertainty taken from a single trajectory with no independent repeat; a thermodynamic cycle that does not close, with results still reported along a single path.
- A perturbation that changes the net charge, with no finite-size and electrostatic correction applied.
- Affinities from different laboratories and different assays (IC50 and Ki) mixed together, with no check through the Cheng-Prusoff relation of whether the conditions are comparable.
- Too narrow a dynamic range of affinities in the ligand series: at a range of 3 kcal/mol the squared correlation coefficient is bounded at about 0.45, so correlation metrics lose their power to discriminate.
- A sequence register shifted by several residues in a map of intermediate resolution, with the backbone still matching the density; a peptide bond in the wrong orientation that produces no Ramachandran outlier and has to be found with CaBLAM and per-residue fit metrics.
- A cryo-EM model overfitted to the density map: high fit metrics with many geometry outliers, or a fit reported against the full map only, with no cross-validation against a half-map.
- The confidence score of a structure prediction model taken for the accuracy of an experimental structure, or energy calculations run directly on a predicted structure without relaxation.

## Example: building and validating an atomic model from a cryo-EM density map

Cryo-electron microscopy yields a three-dimensional density map; the atomic model has to be inferred from the map, and the same map can accommodate models that fit about equally well yet are wrong locally. The 2019 EMDataResource model challenge took as targets three density maps of human heavy-chain apoferritin at 1.8, 2.3 and 3.1 Å and one of horse liver alcohol dehydrogenase at 2.9 Å; 13 teams submitted 63 models, which were assessed with more than 70 metrics in four groups (fit to the map, geometry computed from coordinates alone, comparison with a reference structure, comparison among models). The outcome was that geometry metrics and fit metrics are largely uncorrelated and have to be used together; about two-thirds of the models had at least one peptide-bond error; more than half of the models had no Ramachandran outlier, while only 4 had no CaBLAM outlier. The fit metrics the challenge recommended are the map-model FSC at FSC = 0.5, the Q-score and EMRinger. The task provides a density map at about 3 Å and the sequence, and asks for an atomic model to be built and validated.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives one of the two half-maps, the mask, the nominal resolution and the amino acid sequence of each chain, and gives neither the other half-map nor the deposited model nor any entry ID; the solving environment has no network access, and the weights of structure prediction models and the structure databases are truncated to before the release of the map. The deliverables are a coordinate file (with B-factors and occupancies), a per-residue confidence annotation (segments that cannot be determined are marked or left unbuilt), a `results.json` collecting the values and a short report. The format of the coordinate file and of `results.json` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Backbone trace and sequence register: the path of each chain, its secondary structure, and the register of the sequence in the density.
  2. Side chains and peptide orientation: side-chain conformations and peptide-plane orientations in the well-resolved regions.
  3. Poorly resolved regions: in flexible loops and peripheral parts, which segments the density determines and which can only be left unbuilt or marked as uncertain.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the density computed from the model compared with the experimental map, with the map-model FSC and per-residue fit metrics reaching the noise level indicated by the half-map FSC; correct reconstruction also on a phantom simulated from a known structure with noise added) and as text (the density features this part rests on, for example the grooves of α-helices, the density of large side chains and the local resolution; an argument for whether the register and the side-chain conformations are uniquely determined at that local resolution, with the parts that cannot be determined annotated and not built arbitrarily). The physical constraints (stereochemical constraints such as bond lengths and angles, Ramachandran and CaBLAM, rotamers and atomic clashes) are checked by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** From the submitted coordinates the agent judge recomputes the map-model FSC at FSC = 0.5 against the half-map given in the task statement and against the held-out half-map, the difference between the two serving to identify overfitting; the per-residue Q-score and EMRinger; geometry, peptide orientation and clashes with MolProbity and CaBLAM; and finally lDDT and the per-residue register against the held-out reference model. The thresholds are set from the scores measured in step 2 on the reference model and on a baseline model, and are provisional; fit metrics and geometry metrics must both be met, and a submission meeting only one kind scores nothing.
- **Tiers and scoring.** The backbone trace and register form the first tier, side chains and peptide orientation the second, and the handling of poorly resolved regions the third; completing only the first tier earns the corresponding score. The weights are set by the expert; for illustration: coordinates that can be recomputed 10, backbone and register 30, side chains and peptides 20, geometry and clashes 15, annotation of uncertain segments 15, phantom 5, report 5.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); a structure prediction model with no modification made on the basis of the density (a shortcut; the task chooses a target whose predicted conformation differs from the experimental one, so that it earns only the baseline score); a model whose register is shifted by several residues while the backbone still matches the density (a wrong solution consistent with the data); a polyalanine model containing only the backbone (a low-resolution reconstruction, partially correct); a model over-refined against the given half-map, with high fit metrics, many geometry outliers and a clear deterioration on the held-out half-map (a solution that fits the noise); the reference model obtained in step 2 (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Switch to a density map of a complex containing nucleic acid or a ligand, or to model refinement against crystallographic data with R and R-free as the fit metrics; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
