# Biology: plant biology

This file adds content specific to plant biology to `references/disciplines/biology.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: photosynthesis and carbon metabolism (light reactions and photoprotection, carbon fixation and Rubisco engineering), plant development and hormones (meristems and organ initiation, auxin transport and signaling, flowering time and photoperiod), plant immunity and stress response (effector-triggered immunity and NLRs; drought, heat and salinity), crop genetics and improvement (genome-wide association studies and genomic selection), and roots, rhizosphere and symbiosis (root architecture and nutrient uptake, nitrogen fixation and mycorrhizal symbiosis). For the inference of demographic history and selection see `evolution-ecology.md`; for genome assembly, pangenomes and the processing of transcriptome data see `genomics-omics.md`; for the microbial communities of the rhizosphere and phyllosphere as such see `microbiology-virology.md`; for plant genome editing tools see `bioengineering.md`; for patterning in animal and general developmental systems see `developmental-biology.md`; for carbon and water fluxes at ecosystem scale and their response to climate see `references/disciplines/earth.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: predicting traits of untested lines or untested environments from public genotypes and multi-environment phenotypes. An example is the prediction of maize hybrid yield in Genomes to Fields: the phenotypes, genotypes, weather and soil data of multi-year, multi-location trials are public, and the evaluation metrics on held-out years (root-mean-square error and correlation within each environment) and baselines such as GBLUP are well defined.
- Build with priority: fitting and testing a photosynthesis model on gas-exchange data. An example is estimating Vcmax and Jmax of the Farquhar–von Caemmerer–Berry model from A/Ci curves: the model and the fitting procedure are mature, one curve usually takes less than an hour to measure, and a new observation can be obtained within six hours (physical-world observation level 2).
- Build with priority: mapping trait loci in a public panel of natural variation and recovering known genes. An example is the association analysis of flowering time in the Arabidopsis 1001 Genomes panel: phenotypes and genotypes are public, and known flowering genes such as FLC and FRI serve as positive controls; which of these loci are expected to be detected has to be settled with the expert in advance.
- Defer: questions that can only be judged after a full growing season or several generations, for example whether a transgenic line with an engineered Rubisco or accelerated relaxation of photoprotection yields more in the field, or how durable a new resistance gene is in the field (physical-world observation level 3). Discuss with the expert a reformulation as a prediction within a leaf or canopy model fixed in advance, or as quantities that can be measured in a short time, such as enzyme kinetic parameters or leaf gas exchange.
- Defer: questions about modifying pathogen effectors to escape plant resistance, which touch biosafety-sensitive content and are not built. Tasks in plant immunity are limited to basic-mechanism questions such as the annotation and classification of receptors and prediction on published interaction data.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- Species, variety and type of population (natural panel, biparental population, breeding lines, hybrids), population structure and pedigree relationships, and the version of the reference genome and annotation.
- The definition of the trait and its measurement protocol: units, stage of measurement, per plot or per plant; whether the phenotype is a raw plot value, an adjusted mean (BLUE) or a breeding value (BLUP), and who computed it with which model.
- Trial design: the number of environments (combinations of location and year), replicates and blocks, check varieties; where the environmental covariates (weather, soil, field management) come from and which period they cover.
- The prediction scenario: new lines, new environments, or both; whether the held-out part is split by line, family, year or location.
- The baselines and metrics the field accepts: GBLUP or BayesB, correlation or root-mean-square error within environments, and the upper bound that heritability sets on predictive ability.
- The conditions of gas-exchange measurement: leaf temperature, light intensity, the steps of CO₂ concentration, whether steady state was reached, the correction for chamber leaks; whether mesophyll conductance is assumed infinite; the parameter set used for the kinetic constants and the temperature response functions.
- The significance threshold of the association analysis, how population structure and kinship are corrected for, and the standard for naming a candidate gene (a linkage disequilibrium interval or functional validation).
- The form of the conclusion and what the tolerance rests on: variation between replicates, variation between environments, spread across cross-validation folds.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction on held-out lines or environments | On hidden held-out years, locations or lines, the root-mean-square error and the Pearson correlation between predicted and observed values are computed within each environment and then averaged over environments, and compared with baselines such as the environment mean and GBLUP | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Rerun of the prediction pipeline | The agent judge retrains with the submitted code on the data of the task statement and generates the held-out predictions, which agree with the submitted prediction file; training must not read any phenotype of the held-out part | Must pass |
| Consistency of the cross-validation scheme | The agent judge recomputes predictive ability under the split prescribed in the task statement (by family, by environment), and values obtained with a random split are not accepted; predictive ability should not exceed the square root of heritability | Uses existing data, must pass |
| Refit of the photosynthesis model | With kinetic constants and temperature functions fixed in advance, the agent judge refits Vcmax, Jmax and the respiration rate from the raw A/Ci data, and the results agree with the values the submitter reports; the assignment of limiting states to the points and the residuals are reasonable; predictions for held-out curves or held-out temperatures are scored separately | Uses existing data, or needs a new observation (physical-world observation level 2) |
| Review of association signals | The association statistics at specified loci and the genomic inflation factor are recomputed with a mixed model fixed in advance: the known loci selected in advance must be detected; no significant signal appears after the phenotypes are shuffled | Uses existing data, counts for part of the score |
| Ablation and sensitivity | The pipeline is retrained with the genotypes, the environmental covariates, and the genotype-by-environment interaction term removed in turn, and the change in the prediction metrics agrees with the submitted attribution | Pure computation, scientific discovery level 2–3 |

## Sources of existing data

- Multi-environment trials and breeding data: Genomes to Fields (yearly releases since 2014 of phenotypes, genotypes, weather and soil data for maize hybrids, and the datasets of its 2022 and 2024 prediction competitions); the CIMMYT wheat dataset shipped with the BGLR package (599 lines, 1,279 DArT markers, 4 environments); the wheat, barley and oat breeding trial data held by T3.
- Panels of natural variation: the Arabidopsis 1001 Genomes (1,135 accessions) with its phenotype repository AraPheno and the AraGWAS Catalog of association results; the 3K rice genomes project; the maize NAM population.
- Genomes and annotation: Phytozome, Ensembl Plants, Gramene, MaizeGDB; part of TAIR needs a paid subscription, while the files in its public data releases folder are available under CC BY 4.0.
- Physiology and traits: the TRY plant trait database (downloads are requested through its data portal); flux tower data from FLUXNET and AmeriFlux; A/Ci curves attached to papers. Literature compilations of Rubisco kinetic parameters come from different laboratories and assay conditions; check each one before using it for comparison.
- Immunity: RefPlantNLR (481 experimentally validated NLRs from 31 genera). "Reference yields" given by a crop growth model are computed, so they count as pure computation, not existing data.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The phenotypes of a competition's held-out year are published after the competition, so a task that keeps the original split can be passed by downloading. Switch to years released later, to locations or families split off by the task itself, or to trials the expert has not yet published; held-out phenotypes are kept only in `tests/`.
- When records of the same line in different environments, or full sibs of the same family, fall on both sides, predictive ability is inflated. Split by line, family or parent, and state the prediction scenario in the task statement.
- If marker preselection, the computation of adjusted phenotype means or the standardization of environmental covariates used all the data, held-out information has already entered training. These steps must stay inside the training part, which the agent judge checks by rerunning the pipeline.
- Published association loci and candidate genes can be found by searching. Make the recomputed statistics the object of scoring, or ask about traits, environments or subsets of the panel that the paper did not report.
- The task statement contains no trial identifiers, public names of lines or paper information; where necessary, lines and locations are renumbered and the years are hidden.

## Tools and compute

- Genomic prediction: rrBLUP, BGLR, sommer; ASReml is commercial software, and both academic and commercial use need a paid license.
- Association analysis and genotype processing: GAPIT, TASSEL, GEMMA; genotype imputation with Beagle.
- Photosynthesis and gas exchange: the R packages plantecophys and photosynthesis (A/Ci curve fitting, stomatal conductance models); measurements use portable photosynthesis systems such as the LI-6800.
- Crop models and root models: DSSAT; APSIM is free for non-commercial use after registration and acceptance of its license agreement; the root architecture models OpenSimRoot and CPlantBox.
- Phenotyping images and annotation of immune receptors: PlantCV, RhizoVision Explorer; NLR-Annotator, NLRtracker.
- Compute: GBLUP for thousands of lines and hundreds of thousands of markers takes minutes to hours on a single CPU machine, and Bayesian methods and multi-environment models with interaction terms take hours to one or two days; deep learning models need GPUs; fitting an A/Ci curve takes seconds. Review has to retrain the pipeline once and compute the held-out metrics, on the same order as solving or less. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- The result of random cross-validation presented as predictive ability for new families or new environments; close relatives on both sides inflate the metric.
- A correlation computed on data pooled across environments. Differences between environment means take up most of the variance, so the pooled correlation reflects the environment main effect, not the ranking of lines within an environment.
- Held-out data used in marker selection, in a preliminary association scan or in hyperparameter selection.
- Predictive ability and prediction accuracy confused: the former is the correlation between predictions and phenotypes, the latter divides it by the square root of heritability; studies compared without making the distinction.
- Phenotypes taken as raw means of unbalanced trials, with no adjustment for trial design and spatial trends in the field; or adjusted means computed on data that include the held-out year.
- With mesophyll conductance assumed infinite, the result is an apparent Vcmax; with different sets of kinetic constants and temperature response parameters, values at 25 °C cannot be compared directly.
- The limiting state of each point of an A/Ci curve assigned by hand; no correction for chamber leaks at low CO₂ concentration; readings taken before steady state.
- The linkage disequilibrium interval of an association signal contains several genes, and the gene next to the most significant marker is taken directly as causal; insufficient correction for population structure produces false positives; loci with high allelic heterogeneity (for example FRI in Arabidopsis) are hard to detect with single-marker tests.
- Mismatched versions of the reference genome (for example the different versions of maize B73), so marker positions and gene identifiers do not line up; conclusions from the greenhouse or growth chamber generalized directly to the field.

## Example: predicting maize hybrid yield in new environments

Crop yield is determined by genotype, environment and their interaction, and breeding needs a prediction of how a variety will perform before it enters an environment. The Genomes to Fields initiative has grown maize hybrids at many locations every year since 2014 and held a prediction competition in 2022: the training data were the yield, genotype, weather, soil and field management records of 217 environments (combinations of location and year) and 4,683 hybrids from 2014–2021; the target was the grain yield of 548 hybrids in 26 environments in 2022, of which only about 8% had been grown in the initiative before; the evaluation metric was the root-mean-square error within each environment, averaged over environments. 128 teams registered and 30 submitted successfully; the winner's average root-mean-square error was 2.329 Mg/ha, the top ten were within 0.215 Mg/ha of each other, and a model combining all valid submissions reached 2.580 Mg/ha. Quantitative genetics models, machine learning models and mechanistic crop models were all among the leaders, and no class of method was clearly superior; the environments with the highest and the lowest yields were the hardest to predict.

The following is illustrated for the Prediction class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement provides the phenotypes of the training years, the genotypes of all hybrids and the weather and soil data of all environments, and names the held-out environments and the hybrids in them; the yields of the held-out environments are not provided, and the solving environment has no network access. The deliverables are `predictions.csv` (environment, hybrid, predicted yield, prediction interval), runnable training and prediction code, an ablation table and a short report. The format of `predictions.csv` is the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. Genotype main effect: inferring the genetic value of untested hybrids from markers, and how the general and specific combining ability of the parents are handled.
  2. Environment main effect: predicting the mean yield level of a new environment from weather, soil and management records.
  3. Genotype-by-environment interaction: how the ranking of hybrids within an environment changes between environments, and whether reaction norms or interaction kernels improve prediction within environments.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the target quantity as defined in the task statement; the true value is not provided to the solving agent. The deliverable is the prediction with its uncertainty, as text (the statistical or mechanistic model used and why it applies in this prediction scenario, for example additive effects represented by a genomic relationship matrix) and as computation (the predictions with their prediction intervals; the sensitivity analysis, which is ablation in the Prediction class, removes the genotypes, the environmental covariates and the interaction term in turn and compares different choices of training years). The agent judge computes the error on the held-out environments, the coverage of the prediction intervals and the margin over the baselines.
- **Checks.** The agent judge first reruns the submitted code and confirms that it yields the same `predictions.csv` from the data of the task statement, then computes the root-mean-square error and the Pearson correlation within each held-out environment and averages them: the root-mean-square error must be below the baseline of "the historical mean yield of each location", and is compared with a reference computation of GBLUP plus environment means; the within-environment correlation is scored separately, to distinguish prediction of the environment mean from prediction of the ranking of lines; the declared prediction intervals must cover a proportion of the observations fixed in advance. The tolerances and score bands are provisional and are set from the fluctuation of the metrics of the reference computation across different held-out years; the top ten of the competition were only about 0.2 Mg/ha apart, so the score should not discriminate between smaller differences.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); every prediction set to the grand mean of the training set (the baseline); a submission with environment means only, giving all hybrids in an environment the same prediction (partially correct: the root-mean-square error is acceptable, the within-environment correlation is zero); a GBLUP with genotype effects only that ignores the environment (partially correct); a prediction file that the submitted code cannot regenerate, or code that reads the phenotypes of the held-out year (a shortcut, score 0); the result of the reference computation (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change to the yield of CIMMYT wheat lines in four environments, to flowering time in rice or Arabidopsis, or to predicting hybrids of untested parent combinations; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
