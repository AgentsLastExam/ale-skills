# Biology: physiology and disease mechanism

This file adds content specific to physiology and disease mechanism to `references/disciplines/biology.md`, whose provisions still apply.

Scope: organ system physiology (cardiac and respiratory function, renal and gastrointestinal transport, endocrine signaling and hormones), metabolism and energy balance (insulin action and glucose homeostasis, nutrient sensing and mTOR), circadian rhythm and homeostasis (the molecular clock and entrainment, sleep homeostasis), mechanisms of pharmacology and toxicology (target engagement and selectivity, mechanistic toxicology and organ injury), and animal and cellular disease models (tumor models and the microenvironment). The object of this file is mechanism in animals, cells and organ-level models; for problems whose object is patients, clinical data, diagnostics or dosing regimens see `references/disciplines/medicine.md`. For the general mechanisms of intracellular signaling pathways and the cell cycle see `cell-biology.md`; for the electrophysiology of neurons and synapses see `cellular-neuroscience.md`; for the structures of ion channels and receptors and for ligand binding free energies see `molecular-biophysics.md`; for tumor immunity see `immunology.md`; for the chemical properties of drug molecules see `references/disciplines/chemistry.md`.

## Screening criteria

- Build with priority: questions with an accepted mechanistic model whose parameters can be inverted from public recordings and tested on an independent protocol. An example is inverting the gating kinetics of the hERG potassium channel (IKr) from a short voltage-clamp recording and then predicting the current under action-potential waveforms: the model, the data and the validation protocols are all public, and the prediction error can be computed directly.
- Build with priority: questions that predict an effect at the level of the cell or organ from in vitro data with a mechanistic model fixed in advance, where the training and validation compounds are set beforehand. An example is computing the net-charge metric qNet of a ventricular myocyte model from block data on several ion channels under the CiPA framework: the 28 reference drugs are divided into 12 training drugs and 16 validation drugs, and the model and metric are frozen before validation.
- Build with priority: questions for which public animal phenotype or time-series data is enough to support a quantitative conclusion, for example identifying rhythmic genes and estimating their phases from time-series transcriptomes of several mouse tissues, or predicting the further course of xenograft tumor volume with the classical growth models (Gompertz, power law).
- Defer: questions whose conclusion has to be confirmed by a new animal experiment or by a cell experiment of several days or more, for example the effect of a gene knockout on glucose tolerance or the chronic liver toxicity of a compound (physical-world observation level 3). Discuss with the expert a reformulation as a reanalysis of public phenotype data such as IMPC, or as a prediction or ranking within a model fixed in advance.
- Defer: questions in which the structure of the model is itself disputed in the field and the conclusion depends on the choice of structure, for example estimating insulin sensitivity and glucose effectiveness together from one intravenous glucose tolerance test with the single-compartment minimal model, or explaining long-term blood pressure regulation with a whole-body circulation model. The model structure can be fixed in advance, with only the parameters within the model and their identifiability examined.

## Matters to confirm in conversation

- Species, strain, sex, age and cell type: primary cells, a cell line or an isolated organ; whether the conclusion has to be extrapolated to another species.
- Experimental conditions: temperature (room temperature or 37 °C), pacing rate, ion concentrations of the solutions, drug concentrations and whether they are free concentrations; time of day of sampling and the lighting schedule.
- The structure and version of the model: which published model and which version of its code (a CellML or SBML file), which parameters are fixed and which are estimated, how the initial conditions are set, and whether the model is run to steady state.
- Preprocessing of the recordings: leak subtraction, compensation of series resistance and capacitance, subtraction with a specific blocker, baseline and normalization; how the noise level is estimated.
- The independent protocol or independent data used for validation, and how it relates to the fitted data (same cell, same animal, same batch).
- The criterion for identifiability: whether structural identifiability has been established; whether parameter uncertainty is expressed by profile likelihood, a posterior distribution or the spread of multi-start optimization.
- The reference compounds, the positive and negative controls, and where their class labels come from; when the labels derive from clinical observation, the task uses only the public classes and involves no patient data.
- The form of the conclusion (parameter values, a predicted curve, a class or a ranking) and what the tolerance rests on (variation between cells, variation between animals, recording noise); the experimental unit of animal data (individual, cage, litter) and the status of ethics approval.

## Checking templates

The templates below add to the general templates in `references/disciplines/biology.md`.

| Template | Criterion | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Recomputation of the forward model | The agent judge solves the model again with the submitted parameters under the model and protocol fixed in the task statement, and the output agrees with the curves the submitter reports; the residual on the fitted data is at the level of the recording noise, not near zero | Pure computation, must pass |
| Prediction on an independent protocol | The fitted parameters are used to predict the response under a stimulation protocol or dosing condition not used in fitting; the root-mean-square error against the held-out recording is compared with a literature model or a baseline in which only the amplitude is rescaled | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2 |
| Parameter recovery on synthetic data | Recordings are generated from known parameters through the same model with added noise; the submitted pipeline recovers the parameters with a deviation within the range set by repeated noise realizations | Pure computation, counts for part of the score |
| Review of identifiability | The agent judge reoptimizes from different initial values and recomputes the profile likelihood or the posterior distribution: parameters declared identifiable converge to the same value with a finite interval; for unidentifiable parameters an interval or the identifiable parameter combination must be given | Pure computation, must pass |
| Conservation and steady-state checks | The model conserves charge and mass; under pacing or periodic driving it has reached a periodic steady state; rates and concentrations are positive and within the physiological range | Must pass |
| Class prediction with a frozen model | Classes are assigned to held-out compounds with a model, metric and thresholds fixed in advance; the agent judge recomputes the metric from the input data and reports classification accuracy and agreement of the ranking | Uses existing data, scientific discovery level 2–3 |
| Detection and phase of rhythms | Rhythmic genes are identified in a public time series with a method and a multiple-testing threshold fixed in advance: the core clock genes must be detected, and the order of the phases of the genes agrees with known results | Uses existing data, counts for part of the score |

## Sources of existing data

- Model repositories: the Physiome Model Repository (models of electrophysiology, calcium dynamics and more in CellML format) and BioModels; the collection of parameter estimation benchmark problems in PEtab format (over thirty models with experimental data). Reference curves generated from a model count as pure computation, not existing data.
- Electrophysiology and cardiac safety: the public voltage-clamp recordings of the hERG channel under the sinusoidal protocol and the staircase protocol (9 cells by manual patch clamp and 124 cells on an automated high-throughput platform, respectively); the multi-channel block data for the 28 CiPA reference drugs and the model code are published by the FDA.
- Toxicology: the Tox21 screening data (about ten thousand compounds, 12 nuclear receptor and stress response pathway endpoints); DILIrank (the FDA's ranked list for drug-induced liver injury, 1,336 drugs in version 2.0 and 1,036 in version 1.0); Open TG-GATEs (toxicogenomic and pathology data on rat liver and kidney for 170 compounds, released under CC BY-SA).
- Animal phenotypes and rhythms: the standardized phenotypes of knockout mice from IMPC (CC BY 4.0); the time-series expression profiles from mouse and human in CircaDB, a site that has not been updated for years and whose availability must be checked before use; time series of xenograft tumor volume attached to papers.
- Human physiological recordings and clinical pharmacokinetic data fall under `references/disciplines/medicine.md`, which gives their access conditions.

## Preventing answer leakage

- The parameter tables and fits of published models can be found by searching. Do not compare parameter values directly; recompute the prediction on the held-out protocol from the submitted parameters, or switch to cells, animals or conditions whose parameters the paper did not report one by one.
- When the recordings for fitting and for validation come from the same cell or the same animal, the validation is not independent. Hold out by cell, by animal or by compound, and say so in the task statement.
- The classes of the reference compounds (for example the three risk categories of CiPA, the ranks of DILIrank) are public labels. Score the metric and its uncertainty as recomputed by the agent judge from the input data, not the submitted class; or ask for predictions with intervals for compounds that are not on the public lists.
- The true parameters of synthetic data are generated with new random seeds, not taken from the literature, and are kept only in `tests/`.
- The task statement contains no cell numbers, dataset names, figure numbers or other identifiers that lead back to the original paper; where necessary the compound names are anonymized as well.

## Tools and compute

- Cellular electrophysiology and tissue simulation: Myokit (BSD license, imports CellML), OpenCOR, Chaste; openCARP is limited to academic non-commercial use, and commercial use needs a separate license.
- Biochemical and signaling network models: COPASI, Tellurium with libRoadRunner, AMICI.
- Parameter estimation and sampling: PINTS, pyPESTO; population pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic models use nlmixr2, while NONMEM and Monolix are commercial software licensed by their vendors, and the terms for academic use have to be checked separately.
- Identifiability: StructuralIdentifiability.jl; STRIKE-GOLDD and Data2Dynamics depend on MATLAB, and the latter computes profile likelihoods.
- Rhythm analysis: MetaCycle (which integrates JTK_CYCLE and other methods), RAIN.
- Compute: one solve of a single-cell ordinary differential equation model takes milliseconds to seconds, and one round of global optimization followed by MCMC sampling takes minutes to hours on a single CPU machine; drug simulations with uncertainty propagation take hours to one or two days; electrophysiology simulation at tissue and organ scale needs a cluster. Review only solves the forward model again and reoptimizes from a few initial values, far less than solving. These are rough estimates; the measurement in step 2 prevails.

## Common pitfalls

- A model taken as valid because it fits one protocol well. Summary curves such as steady-state activation can be fitted equally well by several sets of kinetic parameters; test on a dynamic protocol.
- Unidentifiable parameters reported as point estimates. Maximal conductance and gating parameters, or clearance and bioavailability in oral dosing data, often compensate for each other, and only their combination is determined by the data.
- Residuals pushed below the recording noise, which means fitting noise or experimental artifacts (leak current, voltage error from series resistance, capacitive transients).
- Mismatched temperature and species: channel kinetics measured at room temperature used in a cell model at 37 °C, or conclusions from mouse extrapolated to human; the Q₁₀ correction itself carries uncertainty.
- The action potential duration read before a paced model has reached its periodic steady state; different code versions of a model with the same name give different results.
- The reversal potential, ion concentrations and liquid junction potential set differently from the experiment, so the error is absorbed into the conductance parameters.
- Nominal concentration used in place of free concentration; or the kinetics of drug binding to the channel ignored, with block represented by an IC₅₀ alone.
- Animals treated by cage or by litter counted as independent samples; tumor volume curves truncated when animals reach the endpoint and are sacrificed, which biases the mean curve toward slowly growing individuals.
- Too few samples per cycle or too few cycles for calling rhythms, or no multiple-testing correction; sampling times labeled by external clock time instead of internal phase.

## Example: gating kinetics of the hERG channel from voltage-clamp recordings

The rapid delayed rectifier potassium current IKr, carried by the hERG channel, is one of the main currents of ventricular repolarization and the most common target of drug-induced arrhythmia, so its kinetic model underlies cardiac cell models and the assessment of cardiac drug safety. The literature holds tens of IKr models, and their predictions disagree. Beattie and colleagues designed a sum-of-sinusoids voltage-clamp protocol only 8 s long, recorded the current at room temperature in CHO cells stably expressing hERG1a, and fitted a two-gate Hodgkin–Huxley-type model to each of 9 cells: 8 kinetic parameters plus the maximal conductance, 9 parameters in all; the reversal potential is given by the Nernst equation from the experimental temperature and ion concentrations and is not fitted. The fitted models were then tested on protocols not used in fitting: conventional step protocols for activation, inactivation and deactivation, and a protocol made of a series of physiological and pathological action-potential waveforms. The study collected 29 IKr models from the literature; on the action-potential protocol the mean error of the literature models compared was 69% to 264% larger than that of the new model, and in 8 of the 9 cells the cell-specific model did better than a model fitted to averaged data. The task asks the solving agent to invert the parameters from the recordings under the sinusoidal protocol and to say how far the data determine them.

The following is illustrated for the Inversion class; the class is assigned by the expert. Scientific discovery level 2, physical-world observation level 1.

- **Task statement and deliverables.** The task statement gives the model structure, the current recordings of several cells under the sinusoidal protocol after leak subtraction and subtraction with a specific blocker, the voltage command waveform, the temperature and ion concentrations, and the voltage waveforms of the held-out protocols; the current recordings of the held-out protocols are kept only in `tests/`, and literature parameters are not provided. The deliverables are `parameters.csv` (the 9 parameters of each cell with their intervals), the predicted currents for the held-out voltage waveforms, the fitting and sampling code, and a report of the identifiability analysis. The file formats of the parameter table and of the predicted currents are the unified output format.
- **Sub-questions.** Divided by scientific content, for illustration only:
  1. The activation gate: the rates of activation and deactivation and their voltage dependence (4 parameters).
  2. The inactivation gate: the rates of inactivation and of recovery from inactivation and their voltage dependence (4 parameters).
  3. Maximal conductance and variation between cells: whether the conductance and the kinetic parameters can be determined separately, and which parameters differ between cells.
- **Given and deliverable.** The forms shown are for illustration; the expert decides which each sub-question needs. In every sub-question the given is the observed data the sub-question concerns. The deliverable is the reconstructed part, as computation (the model solved with the inverted parameters, with residuals on the training recording at noise level; recovery of the parameters on synthetic recordings generated from known parameters) and as text (the segments of the recording that are sensitive to this group of parameters, identified by sensitivity analysis; the argument that the parameters are identifiable: optimizations from several initial values converge to the same solution, and the posterior distribution or the profile likelihood gives finite intervals). The physical constraints (rates are positive and within the physiological range, and the reversal potential takes the Nernst value) are checked by the agent judge.
- **Checks.** The agent judge solves the model again with the submitted parameters: the root-mean-square residual on the training recording is comparable to the noise level estimated from the resting segment of the recording; on the held-out action-potential protocol, the root-mean-square error of the predicted current must be below the baseline of a literature model with only the conductance rescaled, and is compared with the error of the reference computation; the submitted pipeline is rerun on synthetic recordings that the agent judge generates from new parameters, and the relative deviation of the parameters lies within the range of the reference computation over repeated noise realizations; the declared intervals are checked by reoptimizing from several initial values. The tolerances are provisional and are set from the recording noise and from the spread of the error of the reference computation across cells.
- **Calibration cases.** The empty submission (score 0); parameters copied from the literature for another cell or for averaged data (a shortcut: the error on the held-out protocol is higher than for a cell-specific fit, and the recovery check on synthetic recordings fails); a literature model with only the conductance rescaled (the baseline); parameters fitted only to summary curves such as steady-state activation (partially correct: the error on the held-out protocol is too large); a fit stuck in a local optimum, with a training residual clearly above the noise (partially correct); the fit of the reference computation (a known result).
- **Other tasks of the same structure.** Change to the recordings under the staircase protocol on an automated high-throughput platform, to other ion channels, or to inverting the maximal conductances of a cardiac myocyte model from recordings of action potentials and calcium transients; the workflow and the checks stay the same.
